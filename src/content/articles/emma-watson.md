In the ten years since the first film in the Harry Potter saga, Emma Watson has gone from Hermione Granger, the annoying girl from the Hogwarts school of witchcraft, a nerd who stops time to make it to all her classes, to a fashion icon who picks the right trend at the right time. The eighth film, which will wrap up the series, is about to hit the screens, and Watson will be able to free herself from this part of her career completely. In the meantime she has already signed a Lancôme campaign and shot two films that will erase all trace of Hermione.

To [the premiere of the previous Harry Potter](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/55/ART2/178/790.html), she came in a twenties dress by Rafael López and cropped hair, a dizzying declaration of independence, maturity, and above all freedom from the role that bound her. Her inspirations are shrewd: in interviews she names Cate Blanchett and Helen Mirren as her models, two strong actresses who never gave up an acting career to become celebrities.

The campaigns she has chosen so far, Burberry and now Lancôme, brand her high and right, far from the leftovers of Daniel Radcliffe. "I don't feel I'm leaving Hermione behind, because she's such a big part of me," she says. "I grew up while playing her character, and we have so many similar traits that I feel I'm taking a lot of her with me, onward. But I'll definitely miss her life. It was fun living it."

**Do you feel comfortable in front of the camera?**

"I started acting when I was nine or ten. When you're that young, you're almost unaware of fear. I was lucky, and it came naturally to me. I don't remember much of my life from when I wasn't famous."

## Cool Britishness

Watson is considered one of the most popular and wealthiest British girls in the kingdom, thanks to the Harry Potter series. About three years ago, when she was 18, talk began of her joining the roster of faces for Chanel. In the end she chose Burberry, a brand that tries to project a traditional-but-cool Britishness, previously promoted by style-filled British women like Kate Moss and Agyness Deyn. Watson fit that category like the checks on a trench coat.

At the same time, Watson began to stand out on the red carpets too, and over the past two years has become a fashion icon, with an original, distinctive, sophisticated, daring, and trendy style. In parallel she designed a clothing line for the brand People Tree.

**Do you see yourself becoming a designer?**

"I really enjoyed the experience. There are elements of the fashion industry that can be cruel to the environment and to the people who work in the industry. It's good to encourage people to think about what they buy, but I don't think it's something I'll do in the future."

## A very loyal person

A few months ago Watson surprised her fans and chopped off her locks. "I played Hermione for ten years, so I needed that change. And I've always loved short hair," she explains. In the end, the Twiggy look she adopted turned out to be a wise decision, and over the past year other fashionable stars, among them Michelle Williams and Carey Mulligan, decided to crop their hair. In return, all of them got covers in prestigious fashion magazines.

The campaign for Trésor Midnight Rose, the perfume that will arrive in Israel during 2012, was shot by Mario Testino, who also shot the engagement photo of Kate Middleton and Prince William, and will, it seems, shoot Kate Moss's wedding. He has already photographed Watson for the cover of British Vogue, and now for the July issue of the American edition.

"Mario is someone I trust one hundred percent," says Watson. "After all, he shot me for my first British Vogue cover, and I worked with him on Burberry too. I feel he really understands me, as a person, and we have an excellent working relationship. I think his photographs come out so amazing because he's a warm person with an amazing sense of humor. The result is always beautiful, because the people he photographs enjoy themselves when they're with him. You just feel happy around Mario. He's hilarious, silly, funny, and warm. He never gets stressed."

**How did you decide to join Lancôme?**

"First of all, it was simply hugely flattering. I'm much younger and less established than the brand's other ambassadors. Lancôme is represented by some amazing women, like Julia Roberts, Kate Winslet, Penélope Cruz, women I really admire. They have a lot of style and integrity, and they're hugely successful. In short, it was simply an enormous compliment. I don't know why they turned to me, but I suppose maybe it's because I'm five or ten years younger than the other people who work with them, and maybe they were looking for someone younger. They look for people who will represent them for a long time, and I'm a very loyal person. I think we'll get along great."

**Do you remember your first perfume?**

"Yes. My father took the family on vacation in the south of France, and we visited a perfumer. In the end he encouraged me to choose a particular scent, and I remember my stepmother being annoyed because she thought I was too young to have a perfume of my own. I was seven, but I still keep the little bottle. I've tried to recreate the scent, but I haven't managed."

**Are you a beauty-products girl?**

"Yes! I have tons of products. I've always loved makeup and its ability to turn you into someone else. I don't put much on myself, so it's a bit ironic that I have so many products at home."
