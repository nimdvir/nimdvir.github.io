**Nimrod Dvir, Miami**

Jamie Foxx is on top of the world. And why shouldn't he be? After taking home the coveted statuette at the last Academy Awards for his lead role in Ray, Foxx has managed to settle into the fast lane to Hollywood's club of perks and connections. Sitting, pleased with life, in a suite at the ultra-luxurious Mandarin Oriental in Miami, where he's staying for the shoot of the film adaptation of Miami Vice, Foxx is enjoying his image as the fresh prince of Bel-Air. One moment he's showing off a giant diamond watch that looks as if it was built on the ruins of a mine in Ivory Coast ("I got it for free, to wear to some ceremony"). Later he pulls personal, shirtless photos out of his pocket. "Look at the muscles," he says, excited. "It's all real, really." And finally, to shore up his status as a superstar, he doesn't forget to decorate every other answer with some lethal name-dropping ("So I was riding in the car with Kanye, Kanye West"). But while other stars in this state would have had an obligatory swagger-shaking slap coming, with Jamie Foxx, of all people, you actually feel like being generous. A good guy, you know.

**Jamie, life is good, huh?**

"Tell me about it," he smiles. "It's hard, hard being a movie star. The truth is that what's been happening lately is pretty amazing, especially for someone who came up from the bottom. I find myself in completely surreal situations, like when Will Smith threw a party and I went, and at some point he and Tom Hanks cornered me and started grilling me about how I felt when I got the Oscar. Or suddenly tour buses stop outside my house, and when the guide shouts into the microphone 'And here is Jamie Foxx's house,' a million Japanese tourists pile out in hysterics so they can take pictures by the door. "And there's something else, and it's a little ridiculous, but right now, when I finally have money, I don't have to pay for anything. Like, a few weeks ago they gave me three Cadillacs. Three cars, you get that? So I passed one on to my sister, she cried with excitement."

**In earlier interviews you talked about how hard it is to find love. No need to worry about you on that front anymore either?**

"You could say that since the Oscar I've been having a lot more sex. There are some crazy moments that come with it. For instance, I had a thing with a woman I slept with, and like that, between all the rounds and whatnot, suddenly she moans 'Oh Jamie, oh Jamie, oh... Oscar winner, Jamie Foxx!!!' There are girls in this town, I'd better not get started on it."

The reason Foxx is airing himself out in public and spilling sealed secrets from the bedroom is the round of interviews he's doing for his new film, Stealth, which opens in Israel this week, less than two weeks after its premiere in the United States, where it was crowned one of the

biggest flops of the summer. Audiences, apparently, have had their fill of action productions. As befits a summer movie, this too is a slickly tuned production, loaded with explosions and stunts. Foxx plays an elite fighter pilot, part of a trio of stars that also includes the slab of muscle Josh Lucas (Sweet Home Alabama) and the saccharine bombshell Jessica Biel (7th Heaven). The film opens when the trio is joined by a stealth jet with enhanced artificial intelligence, which they harness for secret missions on behalf of the US Air Force. When lightning hits the stealth's systems it develops a will of its own, dragging the pilots into chases around the world in an attempt to prevent lethal embarrassments. Foxx reports that he enjoyed playing a guy with a bit of an edge: "I play the pilot Henry Purcell, a guy who's a bit arrogant, a bit condescending and a bit of a wise guy. I based the character on reality: I got to meet a few pilots before the shoot, and all of them had that cockiness in their eyes."

**Pilots have the whiff of a hit in Israel, too. How do you explain the phenomenon?**

"I think there's something about them that demands attention, because they taunt death on an almost daily basis, and that puts them in a kind of god-like state. I think that cockiness gets especially strong on aircraft carriers, like the one in the film, because there are 9,000 people on board whose entire job is to support a relatively small group of pilots. In a certain way the pilots are like celebrities, everyone's looking at them."

**The film stresses the camaraderie of the fighters. Were you a tight group during the shoot as well?**

"The truth is I barely saw the other two. Because most of the film is very sophisticated computer effects, the work itself was mostly solitary. We spent most of the shoot in separate rooms, each of us sitting alone in a cockpit suspended 20 meters up in front of a green screen. I saw Josh and Jessica on small video monitors, but during filming my contact with them was minimal. Even so, we got to hang out a lot together in Australia, where most of the sets were located. I can say we put a lot of energy into the work, and about the same amount of energy into the celebrations."

Maybe things were cheerful during the shoot, but now, with the time come to promote the film, a few ego issues are souring the party: it turns out that Josh Lucas was originally supposed to be the film's lead star, since Foxx arrived on the production right after he finished shooting Ray, which means pre-fame. To the great sorrow of Lucas, who had hoped Stealth would be his big breakthrough, Foxx went into the film a small star and came out a big one, so the marketing and publicity effort was shifted to him (and that despite the fact that in reality, Foxx has a smaller part than the other two). Director Rob Cohen explains why Lucas was pushed aside: "When Foxx arrived on set, nobody knew what was going to happen with Ray. You'd be surprised, but originally the part was supposed to be even smaller, but we expanded it after we met Jamie and realized how much talent he has. Later, once he'd become a huge star, we considered giving him more room in the editing stage, and in the end decided to stay with the original script. The only change we made was in the marketing, when we put Jamie at the center, like changing the poster to make him more prominent."

Josh Lucas does try to hide the disappointment, but doesn't quite manage. "What can I do, my name doesn't draw as much of an audience as Foxx's name," he grumbles. "I'm just glad I'm in films that people actually get to see." To which one can only say: don't let them put one over on you.

And back to Foxx. Although nobody expects Stealth to be more than another action movie, Foxx of all people is trying to inject a drop of philosophy into the thing: "What we tried to show is that somewhere along the way pilots turn into players in a sophisticated video game. They forget they're bombing and just think about the flying, so it becomes something completely mathematical. You could say that today the military is turning more and more into a video game. In my opinion, in 10 years there won't be any pilots, everything will be run by machines."

**How much do you now understand what it really means to fly a plane?**

"I think quite a bit. The advantage is that through acting you get to look at other people's lives, and then after the shoot you feel as if you've tried out a lifestyle you didn't choose for yourself. As an actor you basically multiply your life: say you've got 50 years of adult life, then working in films extends that."

Despite the confidence he displays now, Foxx is the first to admit that landing in the cockpit didn't come easily. After a modest upbringing at his grandparents' house (where he moved after his parents' divorce), he tried his luck with a solo album called "Peep This." Following a friend who pushed him to develop his comic talent, he started performing at local stand-up clubs, moved on to television appearances and finally managed to land himself a show on the WB network called "The Jamie Foxx Show." It took him a few more parts in films of greater and lesser mediocrity to really break through, and the long-awaited moment came in 2004 with parts in two successful films, Collateral alongside Tom Cruise and Ray, both of which, by the way, earned him Oscar nominations at the last ceremony.

Foxx is aware of the new status: "I have power now," he smiles, "but I see it as a kind of animal that needs to be tamed. There are times when that animal wakes up, and then I have to put it back in its corner, remind myself who I am and where I came from. I only let it loose at moments when it can help, say when I need to walk into a room and impress someone to get a part. Then I walk in with the pose, 'Jamie Foxx is in the house!'"

**So now, with the animal in your pocket, what projects have you managed to line up?**

"Since then I've worked on a few new films that you'll be hearing about, the current one being Miami Vice, which is shooting right now. I also keep doing comedy, so that if I suddenly want a break from acting, I can always go back to performing at stand-up clubs. If I do go back to stand-up, it'll definitely be different, because the Oscar changed me as a comedian: until I made it, my whole act was laughs on the way up, but what do I do now? Fuck it, I finally have money! So now I make fun of Hollywood, things like that. Apart from that, I'm also working on a new solo album, with Kanye, which will include, among other things, a song about the crazy Oscar night."

**Since we've spent half the interview talking about a statue, maybe to finish you could tell us which room you keep it in?**

"The truth is it's actually at my agent's. You see, my house is like a neighborhood, friends and family are coming and going all the time. When the Oscar was at my place they kept joking around with it, tossing it from side to side, touching it, because it's kind of a thing. Now my manager is keeping it so nothing happens to it. I keep a fake Oscar at home for the guests."
