Some people like to ride bicycles, others play basketball; I like to climb mountains. Six months ago I boarded a flight to Nepal to reach breathtaking heights, among other places in the Annapurna region, part of the Himalayan range.

The Thorong La Pass is a mountain pass that forms the climax of the Annapurna Circuit trek. After about two weeks of walking with heavy gear, through changing terrain, in fickle weather and with breathing difficulties due to the altitude, you reach the day everyone waits for and fears. Before dawn, with frozen water in the bottles and severe shivering, you begin the climb toward the pass. Quite a few suffer frostbite or struggle to breathe, and with willpower keep walking against the body's will. After a walk that seems like an eternity, you reach the highest point, stop and look. The moment when the world seems spread out before you is the moment you came for. There is a power held in these landscapes, a power that even the most advanced cameras cannot convey. No wonder so many flock to this remote kingdom, which lies at the foot of the highest mountains in the world.

There is no doubt that there are things to take into account. Besides rockslides and avalanches, there is also unpredictable weather, strong solar radiation, a shortage of oxygen that can lead to altitude sickness, and a lack of infrastructure for evacuation and medical treatment. There is the risk of developing back and leg pain from prolonged walking under weight. Anyone planning to climb must buy suitable equipment, with an emphasis on a sleeping bag rated for very low temperatures, suitable climbing boots, a backpack fitted to the body and warm clothing. Weather conditions must be monitored, and no one should go alone. One must not assume that "it'll be fine" on the strength of hikes in Israel or combat service. This is another world with rules of its own. Despite these tragic disasters, which illustrate a risk that can be reduced but not eliminated, I have no doubt that Israelis will continue their journeys, all for that rare moment when time seems to stand still.

**The writer recently climbed in the Annapurna range**
