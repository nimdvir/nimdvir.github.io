The monster Mary Shelley dreamed up in Frankenstein, her classic novel from the early nineteenth century, gets another incarnation in [I, Frankenstein](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4480590,00.html), director Stuart Beattie's effects-heavy action film. Instead of the lumbering, slow-witted creature that horror films old and new have served us in the past, this time we meet him as a sharp, agile, muscular hero. Good-looking, even. The American actor Aaron Eckhart was picked to play him, and the film drags him into a war between higher beings: the gargoyles and the demons.

"In earlier films the creature was very big and lumbering, heavy and slow," Eckhart admits in an interview with Ynet. "Because this is an action film, he has to be fast and agile and lethal. He has to think fast, he has to learn fast and adapt, and the film is a lot more dynamic that way. He also takes his shirt off."

And once the shirt comes off, there naturally has to be someone to be tempted by it. In I, Frankenstein that is the scientist Terra Wade, who tries to help the monster help all of humanity in the last stand between the queen of the gargoyles (Miranda Otto) and the leader of the hostile demons (Bill Nighy). The delicate Wade is played by the Australian actress Yvonne Strahovski, familiar from the series Dexter and Chuck and from the action film Killer Elite. "There may well be some sexual tension between the two characters," she says. "Once she realizes he's like a human being, there's a curiosity there."

According to Eckhart, Frankenstein's rage in the film comes from the rejection he suffered in the past. "Where does all this action come from? It comes from the rage of a child whose parents didn't love him," he explains. "The story of this creature's birth: his creation, the father who rejected him, the society that cast him out. He never felt loved, and society treated him badly. I think there are millions of people in our world who feel exactly what our hero feels, and those feelings bring out rage. That's why I think this film is very current."
