New York is bipolar about dogs and their owners. On the one hand, it's paradise for anything on four legs, with some of the most elaborate dog parks I've seen, including fenced-in areas where the dogs can play together on slides, bridges and adorable little treehouses.

On the other hand, dogs are banned almost everywhere, which means that if I feel like sitting in a café with a dog, taking him shopping or riding the subway, I'd better get myself a special bag, à la Paris Hilton, or just forget about it.

Ban or no ban, there's no ignoring the fact that New Yorkers are nuts about their dogs. As befits a world fashion capital, New York doesn't neglect its four-legged residents either. You rarely see a dog here without a coat, shoes or some decorative little scarf.

The shops offer every pattern and color going, and by the five-hundredth time you walk past one of those window displays, any pet owner starts to feel a shopping itch in the wallet.

For a while I figured that Dima, my own personal pooch, could get by without a frilly coat. After all, he's an emigrant from Israel, the homeland of khaki and the pinafore dress. The Spartan approach held for a few months, but when the temperature dropped below minus ten Celsius we found ourselves running through the streets in search of a coat with four sleeves.

On our wanderings we came across Reme's Oggi Pets, a Gossip Girl sort of place (meaning chic) on the Upper East Side, offering spa services for dogs and a couture department.

Honestly, I was sure that the mixed-breed rat I'd hauled all the way from Tel Aviv to Manhattan would not be welcome in a place that glittery, but his charm and sex appeal won over the saleswomen, who looked like the Israeli socialite [Nicole Raidman](https://xnet.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3091418,00.html) with a whiff of dog treats about them.

And that's how we found ourselves, the rat and I, spending a morning of couture in the hands of the two Raidmans. "He's so well behaved," they sighed as they dressed him in a Juicy Couture leather coat ($150).

After trying on a military-inspired hooded coat ($115), a Christmas-pattern sweater ($90) and a special rainproof set with hat and four boots ($190), we said we'd think about it and stepped out into the cold street.

From there we walked a few blocks to T.J. Maxx, a chain that advertises itself to recession fashionistas, which is exactly us.

There we found a coat identical to the luxury set from the boutique, fleece lining and handsome outer pattern included, for $9. On the way out I realized that shopping for dogs is like shopping for people: first you do a lap of the fancy boutiques for show, then you go home with a perfectly good little number from the discount store.
