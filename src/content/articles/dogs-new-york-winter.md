New York has a bipolar attitude toward dogs and their owners. On one hand, it's paradise for those on four legs, with some of the most sophisticated dedicated parks I've seen, including a fenced area where the dogs can play with one another on slides, bridges and sweet little treehouses.

On the other hand, dogs are barred almost everywhere, which means that if I'm in the mood to sit in a café with a dog, go shopping with him or ride the subway, I had better equip myself with a special bag à la Paris Hilton, or just forget about it.

Despite the ban, there's no ignoring the fact that New Yorkers are crazy about their dogs. As befits a world fashion capital, New York doesn't neglect its four-legged residents either. It's rare to see a dog here not dressed in a coat, shoes or some decorative little scarf.

The shops offer an array of patterns and colors, and after the five-hundredth time you pass a window display like that, every pet owner starts to feel a purchasing itch in the wallet.

For a while I thought that Dima, my own little dog, could manage without a fancy coat. After all, he's an emigrant from Israel, bastion of khaki and the pinafore dress. This Spartan approach held for a few months, but when the temperatures plunged below minus ten degrees Celsius we found ourselves running through the streets in search of a coat with four sleeves.

In our wanderings we came across Ramz Oggie Pets, a Gossip Girl-style place (meaning, chic) on the Upper East Side, which offers spa services for dogs and also a couture fashion wing.

Honestly, I was sure the mixed-breed rat I'd hauled all the way from Tel Aviv to Manhattan would not be welcome in such a glittering place, but thanks to his charm and sex appeal he won the hearts of the saleswomen, who looked like [Nicole Raidman](https://xnet.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3091418,00.html) with an aroma of dog treats.

That's how we found ourselves, the rat and I, spending a morning of couture in the hands of the two Raidmans. "He's so well behaved," they sighed as they dressed him in a Juicy Couture leather coat ($150).

After trying on a military-inspired coat with a hood ($115), a sweater with a Christmas pattern ($90) and a special rain-repellent set, hat and four shoes ($190), we said we'd think about it and went out into the cold street.

From there we continued a few blocks to the chain store T.J. Maxx, a place that advertises itself as catering to recession fashionistas, which is exactly us.

There we found a coat identical to the luxury set we'd seen at the boutique, including an inner fleece lining and a handsome outer pattern, for only $9. As we left I realized that shopping for dogs is like shopping for people: first you do a show-off lap through the luxury boutiques, and then you go home with a quality find from the bargain store.
