The conversation with RuPaul, whom I'm interviewing on the occasion of his new television show "[Viva la Diva](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k_bo1UMIiRQ&feature=related)" launching in Israel (yes stars real), opened in a strange way. When I called his office, located in New York, I was greeted by a barrage of shrieks and hysterical laughter, as if I'd barged into a wild drag party.

I was already starting to picture RuPaul, the most famous drag queen in the United States, active for more than 25 years, sitting with sequins on the receiver and a feather boa on the dial.

**What are you doing over there?**

"Nothing, we're just relaxing, resting and looking stun-ning," he says in his pampered voice. "The truth is we're laughing about the journalist who just interviewed me. I've been doing interviews for 300 years and I can tell you, there are journalists who shouldn't be there. He asked me whether I identify with the goals of some gay charity I'm performing for next week. Hell, what am I supposed to tell him, that I have no idea what that organization does? That they hire me, I perform, I leave, goodbye?

"Years ago I'd fake it, mumble something about how terribly important it is that gays help gays. But now I just say 'fuck that.' I'd rather be real."

## On the beach

RuPaul, a machine gun of one-liners, can afford to trash journalists. Since RuPaul Andre Charles burst into consciousness in the early nineties, the 48-year-old queen has managed to do it all: mass performances, hosting series and broadcasts, appearing in films, campaigns, recording albums (including the hit "[Supermodel](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aY0ILbj31Ww)") and writing a successful autobiography.

Now, as noted, he's launching "[Viva la Diva](http://www.rupaul.com/)" ("[RuPaul's Drag Race](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kAe1WO9mEks)") in Israel, in which he presides over a gang of drag artists who want to be America's next drag queen. "For years they wanted me to do this show, but I refused," he says. "I didn't get a promise that there would be no meanness. I would never laugh at contestants on a show. I respect the art of drag too much. This time, the production is by friends of mine and I knew the idea would be safe with them."

**And yet, your contestants are ridiculous at times, especially when they shriek that they've run out of makeup.**

"There's a fine line between laughing at someone and laughing with someone. A while ago I did a project with writers I'm not used to. They tried to write me jokes, and I had to tell them: sweeties, I'm juicy, but I'm not a bitch. People don't understand the difference."

**What's juicy about the show?**

"I'm proud that we managed to create a show that presents drag as an amazing art. The show shows that our body is a temporary thing. You're not a man, or a woman, or black or white. You're not the religion, or the car or the hair. You're born naked, and the rest is drag. That's why this art has survived through the generations. Drag reminds people that there are no rules, that we decide everything, and that you can bend reality and have fun. That's why cultures resist it, because people don't want to wake up from their dream."

**Throughout the show you refer to the contestants in the feminine. Why is that?**

"Come on, we're in the 21st century. It's so meaningless. I call the contestants 'girls' because I respect them. Besides, you have to laugh, life is for enjoying."

**The comparison to Tyra Banks's show ("America's Next Top Model") is inevitable.**

"You can compare. It's a reality show, with girls competing to stay or leave. But that's it. The only similarity is the humor and the synthetic hair."

**And the nails.**

"We had little quarrels, but no crazy 'bitch fights.' Seeing someone create something out of nothing, that's the drama. The real challenge is seeing these creative entertainers do something together. In that sense, we're more like 'Project Runway,' because the drama is in the creating, not in fighting over who took whose mascara."

In contrast to his treatment of the contestants as women, RuPaul himself appears in some of the episodes as a man. "I have to, otherwise it undermines my authority as an expert. You need to see where I start from and where I can take it. I want the winner to be like me, in the sense of representing this art. To do music and television, films, books and everything. In short, drag's next superstar needs to be a spokesperson for everyone who thinks differently."

## Lashes to pieces

The show, broadcast in the United States [on the gay network "Logo"](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3733360,00.html), is considered a big success. It was recently renewed there for a second season, and RuPaul is very proud of that. "Now it's the casting stage," he says excitedly. "Do you want to come? Do you have lipstick?"

**I can buy some.**

"Excellent. You have my word: if you come to New York you're on the show."

**Thank you very much. What else are you doing, apart from filming?**

"I'm on a concert tour, promoting my latest album 'Champions,' which was written inspired by the show, and also writing a book for women that will contain all the drag secrets real women need to know. It's aimed at women who want to maximize their femininity."

**Go on, give them a tip.**

"Here's one on the house. There's a drag-queen way of putting on false eyelashes. It's hard to put them on in one piece. People put them on and look cross-eyed. That's why a lot of women avoid it. What you need to do is cut the lashes into four pieces, put glue on them, and then use tweezers to lay them on the real lashes. That way you get lashes that look real."

**Lovely. Tell me, are you single?**

"Are you proposing? Kidding. I'm a big believer in love. Everyone I've ever had a relationship with, I'm still in a relationship with. But no, right now I don't have anyone. Everyone's in a relationship with me," he laughs.

**By the way, you were in Israel in 2003. Do you think [the drag queens here](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3729320,00.html) have a chance of winning your show?**

"Totally. It's a funny story. I came to Tel Aviv for the Pride events, and originally I was supposed to stay one weekend. Of course the airline lost my suitcases, and that turned out well because in the end I stayed ten days.

I went on trips to Jerusalem, to the Dead Sea, I wasn't ready for such a spiritual and moving experience. The funniest thing that happened to me there was that some girl recognized me on the street, invited me to her apartment and I went. There were loads of people sitting there smoking marijuana. It was a hysterical evening. The truth is I didn't meet many queens over there. I met [Dana International](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3698084,00.html), but she's not a queen, she's a transsexual. I'm sure you have worthy candidates. All you need to be a queen is imagination, a love of beauty and knowledge of pop culture. I'm sure there are Israeli kids with those qualities."
