General Aladeen, also known as Sacha Baron Cohen, is continuing his aggressive campaign to promote his film "The Dictator," and this time he went below the belt: at a press conference in New York marking the film's release, Baron Cohen confronted (in jest, of course) the Israeli journalist Nimrod Dvir, who had come to cover the event for the program "Good Evening with Guy Pines." Their exchange led to Dvir being required by the Dictator to prove his Jewishness, and the latter responded by aiming his gun toward the journalist's groin area. Another performance, an especially amusing one, marking the film's release in cinemas in the United States.

Throughout the day (Monday) Baron Cohen, in the character of the Dictator, moved between various media outlets and scattered all sorts of strange threats, [like the ones he directed at NBC host Matt Lauer](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gip2PuH3mXI). The incident with Dvir took place during the large press conference he convened, in which, alongside real journalists, extras he had planted also took part and asked pre-arranged questions. Toward the end of the press conference, Baron Cohen asked whether anyone else wanted to ask something, and then Dvir summoned his courage and addressed him.

"I introduced myself as Nimrod from Israel, and he immediately asked whether I'm Jewish," Dvir recounts in a conversation with ynet. "I told him I'm not just Jewish but very Jewish, and then he smiled and asked me to prove to him that I'm Jewish. He called me to come closer, approached me with his gold-plated gun, and asked me to prove to him that I'm Jewish while pointing the gun at my trousers in front of all the camera crews. Needless to say, by that point there were flashes going off everywhere."

**And how did you respond to his demand?**

"I asked him if he was serious, he said yes, and then I told him I wasn't willing. He asked why and I answered that I wasn't willing because 'it's big.' He laughed and said 'it must be small,' because if it were big, I'd be willing to show it. I asked him if he was sure, he said yes, and then I opened the zipper and invited him to look. He sort of peeked, and of course all the photographers photographed him checking what I've got going on inside my fly. Then he pushed his gold gun in, as if he were going to shoot it."

Of course Baron Cohen, a warm Jew who loves Israel, did not pull the trigger. Nor did he summon the guard of female bodyguards who were present to neutralize Dvir. In return

for the grand performance, he answered two questions put to him by the Israeli journalist, though he made sure to clarify that this was Israeli chutzpah.

The comedy "The Dictator," starring Baron Cohen and directed by Larry Charles, opens in cinemas in the United States on May 16 and will have its Israeli premiere on June 2 at the [opening of the Tel Aviv University Student Film Festival](http://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4209080,00.html). The film, which also features Anna Faris, John C. Reilly, Ben Kingsley, Megan Fox and others, tells the story of General Aladeen, ruler of the Muslim Republic of Wadiya, who arrives to address the UN General Assembly in New York and finds himself destitute, and old.

It is a supposedly loose adaptation of a book written by Saddam Hussein, though Aladeen and his female guards seem more reminiscent of the former ruler of Libya, Muammar Gaddafi. The film's promotional campaign has included many staged scandals, among them the incident with Ryan Seacrest on the red carpet on the way to the Oscars, during which he [sprinkled "Kim Jong-il's ashes" on the interviewer's suit](http://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4195206,00.html).
