If you missed the first season of Smash, NBC's trashy, addictive musical series (broadcast in Israel on HOT3), you're probably not entirely aware of the world war that drove the plot. The show, set behind the scenes of a musical taking shape on Broadway and based on the life of Marilyn Monroe, gave us a juicy peek into the world of musical theater.

Responsible for the best show in town are Anjelica Huston (as the producer in charge of the money), Debra Messing and Christian Borle (as the writers) and Jack Davenport, who plays the director. Oh yes, and there are also two leading ladies fighting for the glory: Megan Hilty and Katharine McPhee. "We're very close friends," Hilty says in an interview with Ynet. "People always ask me about competition between us, but we're not the characters we play. Our characters, Karen and Ivy, will always have a problematic relationship, but we're friends like two 12-year-old girls."

**Come on, the competition on the show is pretty extreme. It has to affect real life too.**

**Hilty:** "Not at all. Honestly, I've never known two people who were pitted against each other with such intensity. But I don't think it's unrealistic. On Broadway, when you see each other all the time in audition rooms, you know who's competing for the same roles and who's against you. A head-to-head situation like that can arise."

**McPhee:** "Look, I'm known as a backstabbing bitch," she laughs. "But the truth is that in the world I come from, there's no competition like that. I come from Los Angeles, where you go to auditions and don't know anyone. On Broadway I feel it's more of a community where everyone knows everyone, a relatively small group, but in Hollywood the competition is more boring because there's no personal acquaintance, at least until you reach the level of the big names."

If you still don't understand what they're talking about, here's the short version: Hilty plays Ivy Lynn, a seasoned Broadway fox, a natural blonde who is certain Marilyn Monroe is some kind of spiritual mother to her. On the other side you'll find Karen Cartwright (Katharine McPhee), a good girl from Iowa, a rookie in the theater world and, heaven help us, a brunette, who has nothing to do with cleavage and sex appeal, but when she starts to sing, swooning angels fall from the sky.

We meet on the set of the second season's shoot. As befits a prime-time show with Steven Spielberg among its producers, the enormous investment drips from the walls. Hilty and McPhee sit down in one of the rehearsal rooms looking exhausted, the result of filming, rehearsing, recording and so on and so forth. Broadway, you know.

**One of the criticisms of the show is that it portrays Broadway inaccurately.**

**Hilty:** "There are people who complain all the time, but we're making drama, not reality TV. It's entertainment, nobody claims to be exposing Broadway's dark secrets. But people always ask me how realistic the show is, and I answer that in my opinion you could go even further and still stay authentic. The theater is a scandalous world, you see each other every day, sometimes twice a day, you get dressed together, you get undressed together, you sit in tiny rooms, so everyone is terribly close all the time. The closeness is what causes all the action."

Unlike Hilty, McPhee is a complete stranger to the Broadway world, and if it were up to her, she would stay outside it: "I would never play Marilyn Monroe in real life," she admits. "I'll leave that to Megan. I'm not dying to do it either, and I also don't think I have it. I don't have her body or her look. There's no way I'd take the role from Michelle Williams. I would never cast myself."

McPhee likes to laugh at herself and play down her worth, for example when she notes that of the whole cast she's the only one who arrives without some Emmy or Tony on the shelf. Which prompts me to compliment her on her work. "Thank you," she smiles, "I do think I manage to capture the essence of Marilyn, what she was on the inside, but a lot of the time I'm too hard on myself. As an actor, the product is yourself and you feel people are judging your soul. It's a destructive business with 95 percent unemployment, but when you succeed it's the most wonderful thing in the world."

McPhee is being modest, but it's hard to forget that so far her career has been one success after another. True, she didn't win American Idol, but you only have to look at Jennifer Hudson, another Idol refugee who didn't win the prize but has since built an impressive career and is even appearing in the current season of Smash. "We actually didn't talk about American Idol at all," McPhee says of her rehearsal-room partner. "It just didn't come up. She arrived focused and came to work, she was nice but she stayed in her corner a lot. Mostly she was surprised by the speed at which everything moves on this production."

The fast pace is no accident, but the direct result of an attempt to inject youthful energy into the show in its second season. In a bid to draw an audience and make the plot younger, this season brought in a bunch of young, hormonal cast members who take part in another new musical, Hit List, built on a rock model similar to the kicking Rent or Spring Awakening, and even saddled Karen with a torrid romance with Jimmy (Jeremy Jordan), a hunky, tormented songwriter. In addition, since there is currently no one in town who doesn't watch the series Girls, Smash too moved large chunks of the plot to the young world of Brooklyn at the expense of a more grown-up Manhattan.

**Katharine, your character spends a lot of time in Brooklyn thanks to the romance with the broke Jimmy who lives there.**

"That really is a problem, because I detest him," she laughs. "The truth is there was no spark between us from the start, and that is a problem, because I'm a big believer that you can't fake chemistry. Luckily, our chemistry got built over the course of the season, and it looks real, because on the show too the relationship starts off limping and then gets built."

The show's future is currently shrouded in fog, certainly considering the less-than-flattering reviews it's taking from every direction. As far as McPhee and Hilty are concerned, they've already won. After all, Steven Spielberg is their mentor: "Steven is such a nice guy," they gush, "every time he comes to the set there's a positive atmosphere of excitement. He's very involved, it's a great honor to work with him."

**Honor's honor, but it's not certain there'll be another season. What will you do if the show goes off the air?**

**Hilty:** "I've finished recording a new album that will hit stores soon, and you'll also see me on Broadway stages."

**McPhee:** "I'd be happy to do a play in the future, but not a musical. I've performed so many musical numbers on this show. We perform every song in an episode about 30 times, so after it's over I'd rather do something else. Right now I want to focus on television. They say you should save your youth for the camera, so that's what I'm doing. The stage will wait for now."
