1996. The Spice Girls burst out with the hit "Wannabe" and take the pop world (and the world in general) by storm. Strange, but at the time it seemed that Melanie Brown of all people, known by her nickname Mel "Scary Spice" B, would be the one to survive and become a big star once the band had run its course. The big hair, the toned body, and the fact that she could sing (an achievement, relative to the other girls) probably had something to do with it, but reality, as we know, turned out a little differently.

While Victoria, "Posh Spice," who sang roughly two lines during the band's entire run, took the handsome husband (David Beckham) and the big fame, "Scary" settled for the leftovers and never really took off. For the past decade she has mostly been treading water in the fourth division of celebrity: in 2000, after the band broke up, she released her first solo album, "Hot," which got lukewarm reviews and flopped commercially. Even when she defected to the United States and released her second album, "L.A. State of Mind," she didn't exactly break any box-office records.

In that period the British press sneered at her at every opportunity and stuck her with the nickname "Desperate Spice." Mel tried to rehabilitate herself on the Broadway stage with the musical "Rent," appeared on the American "Dancing with the Stars" (and came second), and moonlighted herself silly, but she was mostly remembered for gossip items and assorted romances. The peak was an affair with the actor Eddie Murphy, which may have wrecked her life but did put her back on the map (we'll get to him, don't worry).

Now, as befits a true fourth-division celebrity, Mel B has a reality show of her own. Fashionably late, after the United States, we'll be getting it here under the name "The Real Life of Mel B" ("Mel B: It's a Scary World," airing Wednesdays from February 16 at 10:30 p.m. on The Good Life channel). It's a thoroughly generic show, in which the cameras follow Scary's life in Los Angeles alongside her husband, the film producer Stephen Belafonte, and her children Phoenix (from an earlier relationship with the Dutch dancer Jimmy Gulzar) and Angel, Eddie Murphy's daughter.

"People make things up about us all the time anyway, so why not reveal something that's real?" she explains in a special interview with Rating. "Thanks to the show, people can decide whether they love me or hate me based on what they see. Everything you see there is completely true, far more than the stories they read in the paper. What have I got to lose? Let people judge by what they see."

**It looks fairly staged, to be honest.**

"Not at all. I think it's a very honest show. We're a normal, busy family. Stephen and I go to business meetings and premieres, but we also sit down every evening at a quarter past six to have dinner with the kids. I think people will see another side of me in the show. I'm a very regular person, with my feet on the ground. I'm also not afraid to show my vulnerable sides, or the angry or sad ones. I've always been like that. Oh, and of course there's also a lot of fun and laughs."

**How are the reactions so far?**

"The feedback is great. People love the show and our crazy lifestyle, and also the normal family life we led. All the fans are very supportive. I hope they'll like it in Israel too."

## The lover from Beverly Hills

To Scary's credit, it must be said that she isn't afraid to sacrifice her nearest and dearest for publicity. In the show she exposes her family at its most extreme moments of embarrassment and quarreling, and documents the children's swimming lessons, couples therapy with her husband, and even chaperoning her daughter on a date. Not exactly a healthy environment for tender souls. "Stephen and I decided to do the show after a great deal of deliberation and discussion," she says. "We did a two-week test to make sure the kids felt comfortable with the situation and weren't doing strange things in front of the camera. It turned out they actually really love it. They felt very comfortable, more comfortable than we did."

**So basically you got a family home video in a professional package.**

"Yes, something like that. Mostly I'm happy to see all our kids happy. Phoenix is a bit of a tomboy, very cool, knows how to hit back when she needs to, and Angel also knows how to deliver the goods for the camera: she sings, dances, performs. She's so funny!"

**In the show you zigzag between a ton of activities: singer, businesswoman, entertainer. What's your main and favorite occupation?**

"Being a mother is my first priority. It's the most important job I have. I like being busy and doing lots of things. That's why there's always a lot of action in my life."

Speaking of action, Brown became known in recent years as the queen of the tabloids thanks to her affair with the actor Eddie Murphy. She started dating him in 2006, in a media storm that included plenty of public declarations of love, matching tattoos, wet kisses on the red carpet, and finally, of course, a dramatic breakup plus mudslinging in every direction. He denied in the press that he was Angel's father until a paternity test proved that he absolutely was. She, for her part, said: "The less we talk about the d*ck from Beverly Hills, the better."

Scary claims that she and Murphy are now on good terms. "The media doesn't know this, but Eddie and Angel have started seeing each other behind closed doors," she says. "It's nice that they're able to be in contact and get to know each other. I've said everything there was to say about Eddie in the past. You'll have to watch the show to find out what's happening with us."

**Another guest on your show is Kim Kardashian. What's the relationship between you?**

"Kim is wonderful. I love her show on E!. We run into each other often in Los Angeles. She's a very sweet and genuine person, with a very sharp business mind. We're both busy women juggling a lot of projects at once, so we understand each other."

**You live and work in Los Angeles. Don't you miss London?**

"Life in Los Angeles is wonderful. If I want to go to the beach, I can. If I want to go skiing, it's a drive away. I can also set up meetings about films or close a deal to write a book. I'm a person full of passion. I don't need to work, I love to work. Luckily I get to do all the things I feel passionate about. Life in America lets me not focus on just one thing. Sometimes I miss England, but Los Angeles is my kids' home, and if I want to visit friends, I can always hop on a plane and get there."
