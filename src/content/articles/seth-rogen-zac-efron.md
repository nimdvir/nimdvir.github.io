One of the amusing things about interviewing **Zac Efron** is trying to keep track of the amount of nonsense he releases into the air. For instance, he opens the interview by insisting he knows me from somewhere (I would almost have melted in complete belief, had I not seen him do exactly the same bit for a journalist from Portugal a few minutes earlier). He also says he nearly flew to Israel for a visit last week, but something came up and ruined the plans (again, the Portuguese got a similar story too).

In general, Efron, 27, seems to have no problem bending the truth. For instance, when he insists that the bit at the [last MTV Movie Awards](http://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4510041,00.html), where **Rita Ora** tore his shirt off on stage, was complete improvisation. "I swear I didn't know it was going to happen," he laughs. "There was no preparation at all. It took me a while to understand what on earth had just happened." Interesting. The fact that what he accidentally exposed there was a well-trimmed body generously smeared with bronzer tells a different story, and besides, if the event wasn't planned, Efron absolutely ought to sue Ora for sexual harassment.

**But let's leave Zacky alone**, since it seems the point of his verbal juggling is simply to be liked by everyone, one of the best-known Hollywood traits. He makes a point of shaking the hand of everyone who comes to interview him, smiles with a blinding gleam of teeth, and flirts nonstop throughout the conversation, even when the human version of Godzilla is sitting across from him. Maybe especially then. Efron's approach stands in sharp contrast to that of the brilliant actor and creator **Seth Rogen**, his partner in the film Neighbors, the reason we've gathered here, who seems to have long ago given up the energy involved in trying to get people to like him, one of the best-known traits of a professional stoner. Rogen, 32, arrives at the interview in a tracksuit that looks like pajamas, certainly doesn't flirt with anyone, and in fact comes down fairly hard on anyone who dares to ask him a stupid question.

Both are Jewish (with Efron it's a matter of roots; Rogen is completely Jewish, with parents who met on a kibbutz), both are hugely successful right now, and both make a fairly unusual combination given their film backgrounds, though that is also exactly what separates them so sharply: the first is a hunk who broke out in the hunk role on Disney's mega-successful High School Musical, and right up until very recently (when he has been trying to break the mold with a range of bold roles) was known mainly for his impressive hair and his assortment of abs; the second, by contrast, is a very successful comic actor, a senior member of one of Hollywood's most respected cliques (the one that revolves around writer-director Judd Apatow, who directed Rogen in Knocked Up), and in recent years a successful creator too, together with his writing partner **Evan Goldberg**, the two of them responsible for hits like Pineapple Express, Superbad and This Is the End. That contrast is exactly the fuel that drives Neighbors, the story of a comic struggle between a young couple plus one (Rogen and **Rose Byrne**, who pretty much steals the show as his scheming wife) and a wild fraternity that moves into the house next door, led by Efron and **Dave Franco** (brother of).

"There are a few stories here," Rogen says. "There's no doubt that living next to kids is a nightmare, but it's not only a film about the tension between the students and the family. It's also about a couple dealing with a baby entering their lives. For instance, there's a scene where my character has to milk Rose's character. It sounds insane, but it turns out that really happens in real life. All these stories come to us and we say, hmm, that could be a good idea for a film. The absurdity is anchored in reality. The personal relationships and the reality balance out the million dick jokes. Each of us brings something real to the film. Rose's character, by the way, is based on my wife. In comedies couples tend not to like each other. There's less emphasis on relationships. We wanted it to be real, for them to look like a loving couple."

**What does Zac bring to the film?**

"Blue eyes and a six-pack?" he laughs. "Kidding. It's true that he's unusually good-looking, but what I liked most is that there's something scary about him. He brings something dark to the character, not just a nice guy. Zac makes the students likable, you root for him, but at some point you start to feel there's something pretty crazy in there. There's nothing scarier than an upbeat guy saying 'I'm going to kill you.' It's much darker when it comes from a guy with an innocent look like Zac. We knew he could deliver that. It was obvious he had more to offer. He has the right tools, but he hadn't been given enough chances to use them."

**You said in an interview that before you met him you were sure you wouldn't like him.**

"Absolutely! I knew him as that guy from High School Musical. Only when we met and started working did I realize Zac is a serious actor. We were all riveted by him."

**For Efron**, arriving on the set of a comedy and working next to Rogen was no less stressful. "There's something stressful about being surrounded by fast comedians," Efron says. "Comedy is very hard. Someone like Seth makes it look effortless, but it really isn't."

**Actors report seeing you between takes punching the wall in frustration.**

"I wouldn't exaggerate. It didn't come easily to me, but I didn't get to the level of punching walls. I just improvised less. I stuck very closely to the script. I'm still a student, still learning. I'm trying a lot of things. I want to show a lot of sides."

**Speaking of showing a lot of sides, you're shirtless for most of the film. Aren't you worried that will make people not take you seriously?**

"It's not a serious film, and we didn't take ourselves seriously. It was the safest environment, you couldn't fail. We had such good chemistry that we added shirtless scenes, we overdid it. We felt encouraged to go wild. Every day of shooting on this film was a 14-hour party."

That's how it looks too: Neighbors is saturated with scenes of parties, excessive drinking and drug use, all of which connect directly to the personal circumstances of Efron, who very recently [gave an extensive interview to The Hollywood Reporter](http://pplus.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4515506,00.html) in which he came clean about the rumors and half-reports of his exploits in recent years. These included a battle Efron fought over the past year against his various addictions (the Daily Mail reported that Efron snorted enormous quantities of cocaine, used ecstasy and MDMA, and was addicted to alcohol and OxyContin painkillers), a pair of stays in rehab for drugs and alcohol, the first in April 2013, and several especially bizarre incidents, like the one in which he broke his jaw slipping at home, and the one in which he and a friend found themselves in a fistfight with a homeless man in the middle of the night on the streets of Los Angeles. "He had a knife, the police came and separated us, but it was one of the scariest moments of my life," he said. After the first stay in rehab everything seemed to be heading in the right direction, but then, during the shooting of Neighbors, perhaps because of the pressure and anxiety of meeting such a gifted comic crew, came another fall, during which Efron had to miss several days of shooting to get his head straight again.

**"It's a never-ending struggle,"** he declared in that interview, though he went on to say that today, at least, he has the upper hand: with a steady, calm routine of exercise, healthy food, and bed at 9 p.m., where he reads a script, watches a film and goes to sleep, Efron is beginning to take the reins of his life again. "On the whole, he's doing a good job of it," Rogen said of him. "He was a child actor, and you don't need a sociology degree to see the pits waiting for those along the way." "There was a time and a place in my life for partying," Efron says. "But my party days are over. This film was the icing on the cake. We partied a lot on the shoot, and with that I'm done. I can't anymore, I finished in style. These days I go to sleep at ten. I try to, at least." "It's a story about a transition period that I'm in now too," Rogen announces. "We're evolving from party animals into grown-ups. I don't have a family yet, but obviously the older you get, the more responsibility there is. For me it's actually nice. I'm fine with not partying hard. We're in a process and we're changing. Life in general changes, and you have to be ready for it."

**Would you like children of your own?**

"I see friends who really enjoy it. For me it's not something that's planned right now, no, but I recognize that it does people good."

**Mac Radner, the character you play, is a man of the new, more sensitive breed. Can we identify a trend of men like that in the comedies you and the Apatow crew make?**

"I don't think so. If anything, the trend right now is that **Lisa McCarthy** is in most of the comedies (laughs). What I liked about ours is that the woman is irresponsible too and gets dragged into the nonsense just like the boys. To me that's maybe even a little groundbreaking, which is a shame."

**Zac, what are you like as a neighbor in real life?**

"I'm a fantastic neighbor. I live with my brother as roommates in Los Angeles. If you peeked through my window, I'd probably be sitting on the couch watching a film. **Charlie Day** from It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia is my neighbor. I'm always peeking at him through the fence. So maybe I'm actually a bad neighbor?"

**And the fans don't make noise on the street for all the other neighbors?**

"Not really. More in public places. It's fun to meet them, I love it, but it's very embarrassing, I have to admit. It's flattering and amazing that there are people who follow me, but I enjoy the little anonymity I have. For instance, when I go to see a film and have to take pictures with people in the popcorn line. I'm not always in the mood for it."
