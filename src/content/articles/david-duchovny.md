These days most viewers know David Duchovny as Hank Moody, the hard-drinking, sex-loving, smooth-talking writer from the hit series Californication, which starts its third season this week on HOT 3.

The distance between Fox Mulder, the character that made Duchovny famous on The X-Files, and Moody, the character currently paying his bills, is considerable: one wore a tie, followed orders and worried about fateful questions concerning the future of humanity; the other is shallower, hedonistic, dissolute, artistic and charismatic.

What they have in common, if you must find something, is that both are men who go against the grain, refuse to bow to what society expects and pay a price for it. Loneliness, for one, though both of them pine, openly or in secret, for a woman's closeness.

In real life Duchovny, 50 already, is far more Moody than Mulder. Like Moody, a successful novelist (ret.) who moves to Los Angeles and loses the joy of writing, Duchovny has a small writer hiding inside him. It starts with the genes: he was born in New York in 1960 to a mother who worked in education and a father who was a writer and journalist (and Jewish, going by the name Amram).

He graduated from Princeton in English literature and went on to the prestigious Yale, where he completed a master's in the same field. He even started a doctorate on "magic and technology in contemporary poetry and prose," but never finished it. Duchovny has always written and tinkered with poetry, and on finishing his bachelor's he won a prize from the Academy of American Poets. But his good looks led him to modeling, and from there to acting.

Since the start of his career Duchovny has zigzagged between two fields: science fiction and sex. He first made a name for himself in the lead of the softcore series Red Shoe Diaries. Then came Mulder on The X-Files, and with him enormous worldwide fame. Now he's back in sex, as mentioned, with the lead in Californication.

In life, too, he wanders in and out of the world of appetites. Although he's married to the actress Téa Leoni, with whom he has two children, the tabloids have reported that Duchovny is no small fan of sex, and that in 2008 he checked himself into a sex-addiction facility.

## Willing and Able to Shock

On Californication, too, Moody is a thoroughly sexual creature, and the series has its fair share of nudity, sex and profanity. "We're constantly thinking about how to balance the option of shocking the audience against holding back," Duchovny tells Maariv.

"On the one hand, we're willing and able to shock. On the other, too much of it wears an audience out. Sometimes you don't want to shock viewers because it would wreck the story. It's something we weigh all the time as the series goes on. I think what the show keeps coming back to, again and again, is the relationship between Hank and Karen, the great love of his life. That's really the emotional anchor, and from there you can wander off into the more or less 'shocking' bits.

"On top of that, I think one reason we've always managed to slip in wild stuff gracefully is that everyone knows it's a comedy. The actors play the scenes as realistically as they can, but nobody doubts there's a comic side to it. I think Tom Kapinos, the show's creator, has found the right balance, and we, the actors, try to translate it into performance. It's hard, and sometimes you miss, but that's part of the fun too."

**Were there things in the script you refused to do?**

"Oh, yes. Yes. You know, there were times I told the producers that it's one thing to write something and another thing entirely to put your face on it. I think Tom gets me, and sometimes we have a creative discussion about it. We respect each other."

There's no shortage of irony in Duchovny's life. On the one hand, he stars in one of the most sexual series on television today. On the other, he was publicly crucified when the reports about his fondness for sex in his private life began. On the third hand, he has declared in interviews that he "is not a sex addict" and claimed to be a model family man. The truth, as the eternal X-Files motto has it, is . . . somewhere.

Duchovny doesn't like talking about it, and feels no need to be an ambassador for free love. "Making America sexually liberated is not on my agenda," he says. "Sure, I'm on a show with a lot of sex in it, but our agenda is to tell stories comically and get some emotion in there too. We're a strange hybrid, a sentimental comedy. We have no intention of changing the American outlook on sex. It's there, but to entertain and amuse, not to educate and reform."

Still, it's hard to ignore the resemblance between Duchovny and Moody, whether in the love of writing or in the fondness for alcohol and women. Asked about it, Duchovny dodges. "We look alike," he says, "but his makeup is better. That's it."

**The character's quite a loser, isn't he?**

"Yes, he loses. He loses a lot. But what I love about Hank, and the reason I was drawn to the part in the first place, is that he's a character who speaks his mind. He's a kind of truth-teller among all these people lying around him. That gives him a kind of power, and quite a few people even envy him. It's what we all want to do sometimes, but we can't, because we're afraid of getting fired or dumped, or whatever."

**On the face of it, Hank has a good life. Why is he one of the most tormented characters on television?**

"I don't know why he's so miserable. Maybe because he's a writer who isn't writing. He feels like he was put on this earth to express something, and he isn't doing it. So yes, he has what he wanted in terms of women and family, but something in the big picture isn't working."

**If you met Hank Moody in real life, what would you make of him?**

"I'm a father of two young kids, so I don't see myself anywhere I'd run into him. Our worlds just wouldn't meet. If I did bump into him, I'd think he makes a lot of mistakes and still manages to be okay. He's over the top, almost grotesque, even though we try to play it realistically. I don't see Hank as simply rude. I see him as the little guy fighting the bullshit of life."

**You're considered a good father, by Hollywood standards. You've said more than once that quality time with your kids matters to you.**

"Raising kids isn't simple. There's no user manual. What I said before about Hank's truthfulness also goes for the way he raises his daughter. They have this kind of blunt relationship, in terms of what they have and haven't hidden from each other. As a parent I know you can't judge Hank for his behavior. I think he's just groping in the dark like the rest of us. Parents mostly do the best they can, and so does Hank. I think the lovely thing is that he clearly cares."

## The Ex-Files

Duchovny may be closely identified with Californication right now, but he does other things too. He tries to appear in a feature film every year (though few of them have been much of a hit at the box office), and is currently working on three new films.

"The Californication schedule is really great for me," he says, "especially since I want to do other things, write or direct or act in films. The show shoots only 12 weeks a year, so for me it's basically a 13-week job: I prep for the first episode a week ahead and then shoot 12 weeks straight. That lets me take time off to write, or to be with my family."

**Does it bother you that you're known as a television actor rather than a film actor?**

"No, because I go back and forth. I've done plenty of both. To me there's no difference, except that in film you can make a big pile of money in one go. But what I love about Californication is that I always wanted to express my comic side, and I'd never really been able to do that in film or on television before. And still, I don't think I've ever felt like I'm really, really good. That's probably just not in my nature. I'll always aim higher than what I've done. What I really want down the line is to write more, and maybe grow as a director too. I really want to do that."

**You're also the executive producer of Californication. Isn't that hard?**

"No, because I don't really deal with the practical side of producing. I set the general direction, and then I'm involved in casting and locations and all that. I leave the writing to Tom and his team. Mostly I just try to make sure we're all comfortable on set and all hitting the same tone. That's our magic, the particular style we're in, the safe world we've built on the set, which comes through on screen too."

**And the question that will probably follow you to the end of your days has to be asked: any plans to bring Mulder back to life?**

"No plans right now, but that doesn't mean we don't want to. I'm always happy to play that part and to revisit the world we built on The X-Files. The problem is that the films we made aren't blockbusters anymore. That's always a problem when you want to get something moving with the studios, because at the end of the day a film's purpose is to make money. Either way, I wouldn't want Mulder to come back too old or unfit for duty. I think that would be sad. But I'm not ready to give up yet. I'm not ready to leave the sandbox."
