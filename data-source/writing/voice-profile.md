# Nimrod (Nim) Dvir: writing-voice profile

Version 1.0, prepared October 30, 2026, from the verified Hebrew archive in this repository. Status: **draft for Nim's review**. The evidence record for every claim below is `voice-profile-evidence.json` in this folder.

This document has two jobs. It tells a translator what to protect when carrying one of Nim's Hebrew articles into English, and it tells a writer what Nim's prose actually does so that new English writing on his behalf sounds like him. It is not a formula. When a single article does something the profile does not predict, the article wins.

Everything here is grounded in Nim's own narration, questions, and commentary. Interviewees' answers were read closely but used only to study how Nim selects, frames, and responds to them, never as evidence of his vocabulary or opinions.

## 1. How this profile was built

All 49 solo pieces were read in full. Twenty were chosen for close, line-level analysis because together they cover every genre, four publications, and the 2005 to 2019 span; the remaining 29 served as the validation set used in section 11 to test each claim. The coauthored W106 was read but used only where its observations echo a pattern already established elsewhere.

Source quality: these are complete online editions. Seven pieces (W035, W108, W116, W126, W163, W164, W176) advertise longer print versions that were not recovered, so they are shorter than what readers saw in print. Subtitles, headlines, and captions may be editor-written and were treated as weak evidence unless the same device appears inside the body text.

### 1.3 Separating Nim's words from everyone else's

Each piece was split into (a) Nim's narration, scene-setting, and commentary, (b) his questions and interviewer statements, (c) quoted answers, and (d) publisher furniture (subtitles, captions, bylines, "בראיון ל-ynet" tags). Only (a) and (b) support claims about voice. Quoted answers were used for one thing: to describe how Nim chooses, introduces, interrupts, and answers them.

Three cautions that apply throughout:

- Subtitles are written in the third person about Nim ("נמרוד דביר יצא לפגוש את הגרציה והסתכסך קשות") and may be an editor's work. Several are very good jokes. They are cited only when the body repeats the device.
- Ynet, nrg, Xnet, At, and Israel Hayom each impose house conventions (self-references like "ynet" or "רייטינג", caption style, section headings, the "נ.ד" bracket for an inserted clarification). These are noted as conventions, not traits.
- The earlier AI-generated English draft of W046 was not used as evidence of anything.

### 1.4 Counting method

Two simple measurements were run over Nim's narration only (frontmatter, archival footer, captions, bold prompts, and any paragraph that opens with a quotation mark removed; quoted spans inside mixed paragraphs stripped). Sentences were split on terminal punctuation; words are whitespace tokens. Hebrew words carry prefixes that English spells separately, so these numbers describe Hebrew rhythm and must not be converted into English word targets.

| Measure (narration only, 49 solo pieces, about 17,800 Hebrew words) | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Mean words per sentence, averaged across articles | 14.9 (article means range 7.7 to 32.5) |
| Share of sentences with 6 words or fewer | 24% |
| Share of sentences with 25 words or more | 16% |
| Parenthetical asides per 100 words | 1.1 (range 0 to 3.7) |
| Interviewer prompts counted | 281 across 36 pieces |
| Prompts with no question mark | 26% |
| Prompts of 5 words or fewer | 26% (median prompt length 8 words) |
| Narration sentences longer than quoted-speech sentences | True in 36 of the 41 pieces that have both (exceptions: W007, W062, W080, W116, W122) |

The spread matters more than the mean. A quarter of sentences are very short and a sixth are long; that alternation is the rhythm described in section 3C.

## 2. The core voice in brief

Nim writes like a well-read insider who is amused by the machinery he is reporting on and generous toward the people caught in it. The narration is conversational Israeli Hebrew that freely mixes literary connectives with street slang and transliterated English, often in the same sentence. He watches small physical things (a diamond watch, bare feet, a dog's coat, a full cutlery drawer on a set) and lets them carry the judgment.

The humor is dry and mostly structural: a long sentence of accumulating detail, then a one-word verdict; a comic comparison drawn from Israeli pop culture; a parenthetical that corrects or deflates the sentence it sits in. He is usually the slightly hapless participant in his own stories, never the hero. Sharp lines about famous people are common, but they are typically followed by a concession, and when the subject is grief, death, or someone's real pain the jokes stop entirely.

As an interviewer he is curious about how things work, skeptical of publicity, and willing to say a flat "that's not what I saw" to a producer. His questions are short. A quarter of them are not questions at all but statements left hanging for the subject to answer.

Across fourteen years and five publications the stable core is: concrete detail over abstraction, explicit opinion framed as impression, meta-narration about the article's own structure ("before we go on, some background"), short verdict sentences, and endings that hand the last word to the subject or circle back to the opening.

## 3. Detailed analysis

Each claim names its evidence by article ID. Confidence is marked **high** (recurs across periods and publications), **medium** (recurs within one period or genre), or **low** (observed but thin).

### 3A. Tone and register

**Default register is conversational and informal, with a narrator who addresses readers directly.** Second-person plural imperatives and questions open or steer paragraphs: "קבלו את ג'ים קארי מודל 2005" (W002), "אם פספסתם את העונה הראשונה" (W007), "מכירים יופי מהפנט?" (W172), "שימו לב ל'בלוק'" (W071), "נפתח בחדשות ישנות" (W184), "הרשו לנו להוסיף - ממש" (W195). Confidence: high.

**Warmth and skepticism coexist, and the skepticism usually yields.** The Jamie Foxx opening lists his showing off (diamond watch, shirtless photos, name-dropping) and then turns: "אבל בעוד כוכבים אחרים היו ראויים במצב הזה לכאפת ניעור-שוויץ מתבקשת, דווקא לג'יימי פוקס מתחשק לפרגן. בחור טוב, אתם יודעים" (W001). Jennifer Lopez's acting gets "ואפילו בצורה מוצלחת, ייאמר לזכותה" in the middle of an otherwise hostile piece (W052). Buscemi's arrogance "מעט מכעיסה" but "לא שאפשר להאשים אותו" (W172). Confidence: high.

**Judgments are stated plainly, without hedging, but framed as what the narrator sees or senses.** "אובייקטיבית ניתן לראות שלבושמי... יש פרצוף בת יענה" (W172); "זה נשמע קצת מוזר, אבל יש סיכוי ש..." (W186); "עולה חשד קל שגם להוריה יש יד בדבר" (W195); "יש תחושה שלואיס... תמיד היה בשטח" (W164). The hedge words (עושה רושם, יש תחושה, סביר להניח, יש סיכוי) soften the delivery, not the opinion. Confidence: high.

**Register rises and humor withdraws when the subject calls for it.** The livestreamed suicide column (W087) opens "בשבוע שעבר קרה אסון" and stays sober throughout; the Annapurna opinion piece (W094), written days after a deadly storm, is plain advisory prose with no jokes; the Lyngstad profile (W159) narrates the Lebensborn history without a single wisecrack in the body, and Louis-Dreyfus's cancer is handled with "לא קל לראות קומיקאית דומעת" (W003), nothing lighter. The 12 Years a Slave interviews (W011, W165) are respectful and direct. Confidence: high.

**Tone varies by publication and period.** The 2005 to 2006 Ynet pieces (W001, W002, W052, W080, W062, W079) are the loosest and most slang-dense. The 2019 Israel Hayom pieces (W003, W089) are measured; the wit survives in small parentheticals ("שנראית הרבה פחות, אם יורשה לי", W089) and in which quotes are chosen. The 2017 At interviews (W097, W098) are long, confident, and polite to a sitting minister. Confidence: high. See section 4.

### 3B. Vocabulary and diction

**The signature is register mixing: literary or journalistic Hebrew beside slang, Yiddish-Israeli, and transliterated English, often within one clause.** "כיאה לבחורה שאוכלת עיתונאים כמו פיסטוקים קלויים" (W052) pairs the formal כיאה with a snack joke. Literary connectives recur: כיאה ל, מן הסתם, שכן, רחמנא ליצלן, למשעי, זצ"ל (W071, for a cancelled TV show), "מים רבים זרמו בניאגרה" (W002, a twisted idiom). Slang recurs: תכלס (W071), ביאוס (W062), מבסוט (W001, W080), פאדיחות (W001, W186), לקטר (W058), חפירה (W062, W046), כאפה (W108), פרחה (W079), ניג'וסים (W062), שוין (W052), דאווין (W107), בוחטה (W062), לרדת על (W176, W089). Confidence: high.

**Transliterated English is used for comic timing or to mark showbiz talk, not for lack of a Hebrew word.** "ניים דרופינג קטלני" (W001), "ספיקינג אוף פחד" (W052), "הפפראצי האלה, יו נואו" (W052), "שוקינג." (W058), "פייר? היה מרגש" (W184), "גוד טיים" (W172), "לייב און טייפ" (W087), "ביזנס ופלז'ר" (W046, W163), "און אנד אוף" (W164), "דלישס" (W080, quoting a colleague). The device is denser in 2005 to 2012 Ynet/nrg work and nearly absent in Israel Hayom 2019. Confidence: high for presence, medium for the chronological thinning.

**Israeli cultural references serve as comic yardsticks, and they assume an Israeli reader of that moment.** Zehava Ben (W062), Pnina Rosenblum (W062 and again W184), Sarit Hadad and the Hadera municipality (W052), Tami Ben Ami (W079), Ora Namir's haircut (W052), Nicole Raidman (W107), Miri Regev and Irit Rahamim (W097), Einav Bubil (W087), Burger Ranch (W079), Sucrazit (W062), Ya'arot HaCarmel (W093), the film "כלבת" (W093), Jonathan Pollard (W158), Roni Leibovitz (W186). These date quickly and are the single hardest thing to carry into English (section 6). Confidence: high.

**Concrete over abstract.** Prices in a list ($150, $115, $90, $190 and then $9, W107); crew counts and hours ("150 איש... מ-8:00 בבוקר עד 22:00... סצנה אחת בלבד (!)", W158); the contents of a prop drawer; "20 בקבוקים" a day (W080, quoting). When he generalizes, it is one sentence drawn from a concrete scene, not a paragraph of theory. Confidence: high.

**Evaluative language runs through compressed epithets and mock-honorifics.** "פצצת הסכרין ג'סיקה ביל", "גוש השרירים ג'וש לוקאס" (W001); "הגרציה", "הגברת" (W052); "הליידי" (W004); "המלכה בת ה-48" (W006); "כלבות בידור", "דודות מנופחות שיער" (W080); "החתיך ההוליוודי" (W186); "ילד האהבה הטראשי" for his own column (W062). Diminutives carry affectionate mockery: כלבלב, צעיפון (W107), מאהבון (W164), ילדון (W080), זאקי (W176). Confidence: high.

**Distinctively his rather than generic Hebrew journalese:** the register collision inside one sentence; the mock-honorific for the subject; the one-word English verdict; "(קחו אוויר)" before a long list, which appears in both nrg (W159, "קחו אוויר, זה ארוך") and Ynet (W163, "היא (קחו אוויר) מפיקה"). Generic to the trade and not evidence of him: "בראיון ל-ynet", age in parentheses after the name, "כאמור", "כזכור".

### 3C. Syntax and sentence rhythm

**Long accumulation, then a short verdict.** This is the most reliable rhythmic signature. "...וכמובן, כל סט צילום שכזה נגמר בסקס סוער. שוקינג." (W058). A paragraph on Emma Stone's humiliating audition ends "אאוץ'." (W195). "למה? כי גברים הם מצרך יותר נדיר ומבוקש בשוק הדייטינג האכזרי. קשה." (W035). "...ואלה שצורחים 'אתם עוזרים לצלם את השטן'. נו, שוין." (W052). "סוג של שיחוק." (W184). "לא רע." (W171). "אז מה." (W079). "ובכל זאת, זה לא באמת זה." (W062). The measurements bear this out: a quarter of narration sentences are six words or fewer while a sixth run past 25. Confidence: high.

**Parenthetical asides are frequent and do real work.** About one per hundred words of narration. They explain for an Israeli reader ("(טלפון נייד שמחבר אתכם באופן יעיל וחונק לתיבת המייל שלכם. יופי לכם שאתם לא יודעים)", W062, on the BlackBerry in 2005), confess ("(ראיתי תמונות)", W164; "(אנחנו פולנים, מתרגלים לזה)", W062), insist on accuracy ("(בחיי שזה השם...)", "(שוב, לא ממציא)", W092), deflate ("(ולא, לדמות הראשית לא קוראים ישראל)", W058; "(אח של)", W176), or deliver the joke ("(ומי שלא, תמיד אפשר ללכת להתחבא אצל הבנות...)", W079). Density is highest in the editorial column W079 (3.7 per 100 words) and lowest in the sober pieces (W071, W094: none). Confidence: high.

**Rhetorical questions pivot a paragraph.** "ולמה זה פרס?" (W080), "ולמה שלא יהיה?" (W001), "מה אפשר להפסיד?" (W092), "אז למה להוציא את עצמך בכוח מהכלל?" (W071), "מה הם עשו במשך 14 שעות כפול 3?" (W158), "האם מישהו זקוק להוכחות נוספות?" (W195). Confidence: high.

**Colon and dash as setup punctuation.** "הסוף היה עגום:" (W087), "התוצאה - פריקת אגרסיות הדדיות" (W052), "תזכורת לעצמי - הילדים בישראל קשוחים" (W079), "כמו סוכרזית - זה אולי עוזר להמתיק את הקפה, אבל זה תמיד יישאר סוג של תחליף" (W062). Numbered lists appear inside prose when the point is a tally: W195 opens with "1. ... 2. ... 3. ... 4.", W184 lists "1." and "2." problems with reality TV. Confidence: high.

**Paragraphs are short to medium (two to five sentences) and end on their strongest clause.** Single-sentence paragraphs are rare and reserved for a beat ("'הגזמתי', מספר מייקל", W164). The joke or the judgment is almost always the final clause of the paragraph, not the middle. Confidence: high.

**Hebrew-specific features that will not transfer literally:** pronoun-dropping, present-tense narration of interviews ("היא אומרת", "מחייך", "נאנחת"), chains of "ש" clauses, and verb-first word order. Section 6 handles these.

### 3D. Humor, irony, and sarcasm

**The comic comparison is the dominant device.** Buscemi's face "מתעוות כמו שממית לאחר חימום קל במיקרו" and interviewing him is "קל כמעט כמו לחלוב מיץ תפוזים מפרה" (W172). Preparing to meet Michael Lewis felt "כמו דמי מור שמנסה לפתות מאהבון צעיר" (W164). Leon "רכב על קלף ההומוסקסואל כמו בוקרת בקריז" (W071). The Israeli community in New York is "קצת כמו סוכרזית" (W062). Being caught in a Homeland meeting room: "חטפתי על כך צרחות משל הייתי ג'ונתן פולארד" (W158). Randy Harrison's arrival "יהיה כמו לזרוק סטייק לכלוב של אריות" (W118). Gaga's speech: "ליאונרדו דה וינטג'" (W004). Confidence: high.

**Deadpan understatement and mock-formality.** "לא נרשמה אפילו הפרחת נשיקה אחת לרפואה" (W052); "מדונה הוא עדיין לא" (W172); "לא הייתי אומר שתהיה פה מתיחת פנים, אבל אולי איזו זריקת בוטוקס לאזור הטי" (W079); "החיים בתחום הפורנו הגאה אכן לא פשוטים, אבל גם לא משהו שצריך לקטר עליו לאמא" (W058). Confidence: high.

**Self-directed humor is frequent and the narrator is the foil.** He is "כחתלתול מוכה" after a J.Lo glare (W052); "בלב רועד עליתי למיקרופון" (W052); "אני אינני אצן, ולמעשה מתחזק תדמית של פדלאה רצינית" (W114); the Buddhism class dissolves into his own laughing fit and ends "עם כל הכבוד, אי אפשר לקחת אותו לשום דבר רציני" about his friend, after admitting he could not stop either (W092); his only word in ten silent days was "שיט" as he fell into a snow pit (W093); "כמעט הייתי נמס באמונה מוחלטת" at Zac Efron's recycled charm (W176); "פאשניסטות מיתון, שזה בדיוק אנחנו" (W107). Confidence: high.

**Humor aimed at others targets pretension and machinery, then usually concedes.** Lopez's entourage and lateness (W052), Gaga's self-important pauses (W004), Efron's borrowed anecdotes (W176), the staged "reality" of a reality show (W184), celebrity journalists as "מורמוריות" (W080). The sharp line is typically followed by a softening: "מקסימום חמודה" (a colleague's verdict on Alba, W080), "ייאמר לזכותה" (W052), "אבל בואו נניח לזאקי" (W176). The Buscemi piece is the exception where the joke is sustained, and it is framed as inverting the convention of opening a profile with a beauty description. Confidence: high for the pattern, medium for the "always concedes" claim (W052 concedes only briefly).

**Setup and punchline placement.** The setup is a list or an escalating sentence; the punchline is the final short clause or a one-word sentence. In W092 the escalation runs "התחיל לצחקק. ואז לצחוק. ולצחוק. ואז גם אני התחלתי לצחוק." and the release is the fart ("ללואיס ברח נאד"), itemized as three consequences. In W107 four boutique prices are listed, then "ב-9 דולר בלבד". Confidence: high.

**Where humor disappears.** W087, W094, W159 (body), W011, W165, W122, the cancer passages of W003, the Lebensborn passages of W159, and the #MeToo passage of W098. Headlines and subtitles may still joke in those pieces (W159's subtitle plays on "אבא של זאת מאבבא"), but Nim's body text does not. Confidence: high.

**Headline wordplay** (ג'יי לא, מגילת אסתר, בדיחה של מאסטר, סופר סטאר, מהראג'ה, TO B, SEX FILES, תקופת הסטון, לבן בחלום שחור) recurs across four publications, which makes it likely to be at least partly his, but headlines are often editorial. Confidence: low as a personal trait.

### 3E. Storytelling and structure

**Four opening strategies recur.**

1. A physical scene with one telling detail: Julianne Moore is exhausted and asks for mercy "מלווה בעננת בושם מתקתקה" (W046); Foxx in a Miami suite flashing a diamond watch "שנראה כאילו נבנה על חורבותיו של מכרה בחוף השנהב" (W001); Louis-Dreyfus kicks off her heels and edits on a laptop in a hotel corridor (W003).
2. A general proposition immediately undercut: "פעם אמרו לנו שהפשע לא משתלם. מצד שני, פעם גם אמרו לנו שג'ים קארי הוא מטורף" (W002); "אחת הקלישאות הגדולות קובעת ש... אחרי ליווי יום צילום... אפשר לסכם זאת כך" (W058); "זו אומנם קלישאה, אבל היא מוכיחה את עצמה" (W092).
3. Direct address or an instruction to the reader: "קבלו את", "אם פספסתם", "מכירים יופי מהפנט?", "נפתח בחדשות ישנות".
4. The subject's own words first: W116, W163, W185, and the boxed quote that opens W052.

Confidence: high.

**Character introductions follow a pattern: age and credentials, one physical or behavioral detail, a comic epithet, then a quote.** "אלבה, בקושי בת 24, היא כבר שועלת מקצוע... דופקת כניסה דרמטית תוך שהיא מלמלת 'קר-פה-קר-פה-קר-פה'" (W080). Broderick arrives "נבוך ומחוייך... עם כרס עגלגלה (ודי גדולה) ושיער בעיצוב של 'התעוררתי, העברתי יד והרי אני כאן'" (W023). Rashid "דופק איחור של שעה לריאיון, למרות שהוא גר דלת ליד" (W097). Confidence: high.

**Meta-narration about the article's own structure is a signature.** "לפני שממשיכים לחפור בגברת, קצת חומר רקע" (W046); "לפני שממשיכים, חייבים להסביר איך הפכה..." (W004); "אם עדיין לא הבנתם על מה הן מדברות, הנה ההסבר בקצרה" (W007); "אבל למה להקדים את המאוחר. נתחיל בפרמיירה" and "ונחזור למציאות" (W052); "ונחזור לפוקס" (W001); "על ליידי באני כבר הרחבנו" (W184); "ואם זה לא מספיק" (W195). He tells the reader where the piece is going and then goes there. Confidence: high.

**Movement between observation, background, quote, and commentary is quick and explicit.** A typical interview paragraph: a line of narration that places the subject, a quote, a one-clause reaction. Background paragraphs are announced (above) and kept short. Commentary sits at paragraph ends. Confidence: high.

**Context for Israeli readers is supplied inline, usually in parentheses or a "נ.ד" bracket.** Enron explained (W002), the BlackBerry (W062), "שוד המגדל" as the real meaning of "Tower Heist" (W023), Jesse Eisenberg identified (W195), the Academy identified (W174). Confidence: high; the bracket form is a trade convention, the habit of glossing is his.

**Endings come in three kinds, none of them a grand conclusion.**

1. The subject's last line as the punchline: Broderick offering a doorman job (W023), Gosling singing and dancing shirtless "תמורת הסכום הנכון" (W186), Harrison's "אין לאף אחד סיכוי איתי" (W118), Lewis solving Obama's economy (W158), the sausage factory (W003), Rashid sticking out his tongue in pink (W097).
2. A callback to the opening: W080 closes "ככה זה, מלחיץ להיות עיתונאית בידור" after opening on how miserable entertainment journalists are; W052 ends by naming Lopez a Don Corleone after the subtitle promised a quarrel; W092 ends "בלי לואיס".
3. A modest moral from a small event, in the columns: shopping for a dog is like shopping for people (W107); New York itself can be a vipassana (W093); "זהו, אני את שלי פרקתי, לפחות עד השנה הבאה" (W062).

Confidence: high.

**Structure by genre.** Interviews alternate narration and Q&A with announced background breaks. Columns are a single narrative arc with a small conclusion. Features (W158, W184, W056) are reportage with "I was there" markers and a quoted-voices middle. The Lyngstad profile (W159) and the Stone profile (W195) are researched chronologies with section headings and a thin narrator. The one solo review (W114) runs: epigraph, context, personal admission, assessment, measured verdict, bibliographic line. Confidence: high for interviews and columns, medium for features and reviews given sample size.

### 3F. Interviewing style

**Rapport is built on shared ground and light flattery.** NYU days with Gaga (W004), the Israeli angle (Shalit visiting the Homeland set, W158; "מה דעתך על ישראל", W003; Jewish roots, W176), a compliment to McPhee that earns a thank-you (W007), "בחורה כלבבי" about Patti Stanger (W035). Confidence: high.

**Prompts are short and often not questions.** Median eight words; a quarter are five words or fewer; a quarter carry no question mark. "והציפורניים." (W006), "ומלאי ממון." (W004), "רק אהבה?" (W080), "למה בעצם?" (W002), "מכות הלכת?" (W108), "אתה יודע שחם אצלנו." (W162). Statement-prompts put an observation on the table and wait: "זה נראה די מבוים, למען האמת." (W126); "דווקא עושה רושם שבאופן יחסי התקשורת האמריקאית רכה איתך." (W046); "הלו, גם אתה מחשב את השכר שלך במיליונים." (W002). Confidence: high.

**Follow-ups pick up the subject's own word and turn it.** Moore says she chooses roles that are not what she has done before; the next prompt begins "לא עשית בעבר, ובכל זאת..." and when she begs to be asked her favorite color instead of about motherhood, he asks "מה הצבע האהוב עלייך?" (W046). Hedda Lettuce says the other queens hate her, then says they don't: "את לא? הרגע אמרת שכן." (W184). "כבוד כבוד, אבל לא בטוח שתהיה עוד עונה" (W007). "כן, ועדיין הוא לא הכוכב" (W023). Confidence: high.

**Direct challenge is stated, not performed.** "לא בדיוק. במו עיניי ראיתי שצילמתם שוב..." to the reality producer (W184); "יש פה משהו. מצד אחד אתה לא כשיר לצבא, ומצד שני אתה מפזז..." (W164); "ומה בנוגע לעובדה שהמסעדה נסגרה לצורך העניין בצהריים?" (W184). Confidence: high.

**Playful provocation and flirtation are part of the toolkit.** "תגיד, אתה פנוי?" to RuPaul (W006); "אם זה לכל המשפחה, כולל אשתך, אז יש מצב" (W023); "הכנת כבר את הטוקסידו לאוסקר?" (W011); "אתה מתכנן לנצל את הקשר?" after Obama (W158). Confidence: high, concentrated in entertainment pieces; absent with the minister (W098) and the 12 Years a Slave cast.

**Evasion and rebuke are recorded, then interpreted in narration rather than fought in the moment.** Lopez's "אין תגובה" is printed and the piece then reads her as a businesswoman (W052). Moore's "די, בואו נדבר על הסרט" gets "בוא נדבר באמת" and a pivot to the film (W046). Sandra Oh's scolding ("אתה טריקי") is reported with evident enjoyment, and the narration explains her habit of grading journalists' questions (W089). Mendes's "זו שאלה אישית מדי" is printed without comment (W021). Confidence: high.

**Balance.** Curiosity about mechanics (how a writers' room runs, W010; how an Israeli series is sold to HBO, W163; how reality TV is staged, W184) sits beside skepticism of PR, empathy when the subject is exposed (Rada's phone call asking not to be made a criminal, honored with "והאמת, מגיע לה", W108; Efron's relapse, W176; Louis-Dreyfus, W003), and occasional performance (the press-table chorus, W080). Confidence: high.

### 3G. Authorial presence and reader relationship

**First person singular in interviews and columns; first person plural for "we Israelis" or "we readers."** "גם אלינו היא תגיע" (W004), "אצלנו" (W010), "מגיעים שוב למסקנה" (W062). The narrator is physically inside the scene (sneaking into a meeting room, W158; sitting on a drag queen's couch, W184; hosting Lewis at 11 p.m., W164) but as observer, not protagonist. Confidence: high.

**Personal disclosure is small and functional.** Polish family and take-away holiday meals (W062), the dog Dima from Tel Aviv (W107), NYU with Gaga (W004), the move back to Israel and a sunburn (W079), the laughing fit and the silent retreat (W092, W093), mountain trekking (W094). Each fact serves the point being made; none becomes the subject of the piece except in the personal columns, and even there the self is treated with mockery. Confidence: high.

**Judgment is explicit but offered as impression, and interpretation is handed back to the reader at the end.** "ואולי בעצם הסכיזופרניה הזאת היא סוד קסמה" (W052); "ואולי הווייב הקפדן הוא בעצם הדרך של או... להתענג על מעמד הסופרסטארית" (W089). He rarely ends with a thesis; he ends with a line and lets it sit. Confidence: high.

**Rhetorical questions are used for pacing, not for sermonizing** (examples in 3C). The opinion columns (W071, W087) do argue, but in two or three plain sentences ("אני לא אומר שצריך להחביא את הנטיות... הבעיה מתחילה כשאנו הופכים את עצמנו לקוריוז", W071). Confidence: high.

### 3H. Genre and historical variation

What stays stable across 2005 to 2019 and five outlets: concrete detail; explicit impression-framed judgment; announced structural turns; short verdict sentences after long ones; parenthetical asides; endings that give the subject the last line or call back; statement-prompts and short follow-ups; humor withdrawn for grief or real harm.

## 4. Stable traits versus context-dependent traits

**Stable (carry into every translation and every new piece):**

- Concrete, observed detail does the evaluating.
- Explicit opinion, framed as what the narrator sees ("עושה רושם", "יש תחושה").
- Sentence rhythm that alternates long accumulation with a short verdict.
- Parenthetical asides that correct, confess, gloss, or deflate.
- Announced structural turns ("before we go on", "back to").
- Endings that give the subject the last word or circle back; no summarizing paragraph.
- Short prompts; statements used as prompts; follow-ups that reuse the subject's word.
- The narrator as a slightly hapless participant, never the hero.
- Humor withdrawn when the subject is grief, illness, or real harm.

**Context-dependent (match to the article or the brief, do not import by default):**

- Density of slang and transliterated English (high in 2005 to 2012 entertainment pieces, low in 2017 to 2019 and in opinion).
- Israeli pop-culture yardsticks (dense in columns and early interviews; rare in Israel Hayom).
- Flirtatious or provocative prompts (entertainment interviews only).
- Mock-honorifics for the subject (celebrity pieces, not politicians or grieving people).
- Sustained comic description of a person (W172 is the only extended case).
- First-person plural "we Israelis" framing (depends on outlet and audience).

## 5. Genre-specific guidance

**Celebrity interview.** Open on a scene detail or a proposition and its undercut. Introduce the subject with age, credentials, one physical or behavioral detail, and a quote. Keep narration between answers to one or two sentences. Announce the background paragraph and keep it short. Let a challenging prompt be a statement. End on the subject's best line or a callback. Keep the synopsis paragraph plain; the jokes live around it, not in it.

**Personal column.** One arc, one small event, the narrator as foil. Allow one escalation sequence. End with a modest observation drawn from the event, one or two sentences, never a lesson about life in general. Israeli references are welcome here when the audience is Israeli; otherwise gloss lightly or choose an observed detail instead.

**Opinion.** Two or three plain declarative sentences carry the position. Examples are concrete and named. Humor is permitted in the examples, not in the thesis. When the subject involves death or harm, no humor at all (W087, W094).

**Feature and set visit.** "I was there" markers (what he saw in a drawer, how many hours, who shouted at him). Quoted voices do the arguing; the narrator frames. A numbered tally is fine when the point is a count.

**Researched profile.** Thin narrator, section headings, chronology; keep one or two dry parentheticals and nothing more (W159, W195).

**Review.** Epigraph or context, a personal admission that positions the reviewer, assessment in concrete terms, measured verdict, and a plain closing. Sample is one piece; treat as provisional.

## 6. Carrying the voice into English

### 6.1 What transfers directly

- Sentence rhythm: long accumulation then short verdict. English handles one-word sentences well. "Shocking." "Ouch." "Tough." "Fair enough."
- Parentheses as asides. English readers accept them at the same density.
- Explicit impression-framed judgment ("it's hard to shake the feeling that", "there is a sense that").
- Announced structural turns ("Before we go on, some background." "Back to Foxx.").
- Statement-prompts in bold without a question mark.
- Mock-honorifics ("Her Grace", "the lady", "the Queen, 48").
- Concrete lists and prices.
- The subject's last line as the ending.

### 6.2 What needs adaptation

**Syntax.** Hebrew drops pronouns and fronts verbs; English needs subjects. Long "ש" chains become two English sentences, but the punchline must stay in the final clause. Present-tense narration ("she says", "he smiles") is natural in English magazine prose and should be kept rather than shifted to past tense.

**Transliterated English in the Hebrew.** When Nim writes "שוקינג" or "ספיקינג אוף", the comedy is partly in the code-switch. In English the word simply becomes English and the switch disappears. Do not compensate by inventing a new joke. Keep the informality of the surrounding sentence and accept that this layer is lost; note it in the review file when it matters.

**Israeli references.** Keep the reference. Add a two- or three-word gloss only when the joke depends on knowing who the person is ("Zehava Ben, the Mizrahi ballad singer"). Never substitute an American or British equivalent; a reader who meets Pnina Rosenblum in an English text is meeting Nim's actual frame of reference. If a gloss would kill the line, keep the line unglossed and record the choice.

**Slang.** Match register, not dictionary meaning. תכלס becomes "honestly" or "bottom line", not "in practice". ביאוס is "a general funk". לקטר is "grumble". חפירה is "digging" or "going on about it". Avoid slang that is strongly British, strongly American, or dated in a way the Hebrew is not.

**Idioms and twisted idioms.** "מים רבים זרמו בניאגרה" is a deliberate twist on the standard idiom; render it with an equally light twist ("a lot of water has gone over Niagara") rather than the flat "much water has flowed under the bridge".

**Yiddish-Israeli words.** בלבוסטע can stay as "balabusta" with context (Tishby defines it herself in W163). שוין is "oh well" or "so it goes". וייברעס / "מורמוריות" become "the aunties" or "the biddies", keeping the affectionate mockery.

**Humor and timing.** Keep the setup long and the punch short. Do not add intensifiers. Do not explain a joke in a following sentence. If a comic comparison is culturally opaque, translate it literally and gloss minimally; do not replace it with a different comparison.

**Understatement and sarcasm.** Preserve the mild form. "לא רע" is "Not bad." not "Impressive." "מדונה הוא עדיין לא" is "He is not Madonna yet." Do not escalate skepticism into hostility; Nim's sharp lines are usually followed by a concession, and the concession must be translated with the same weight.

**Directness.** Hebrew prompts like "הלו, גם אתה..." are blunt but friendly. English "Hold on, you also..." keeps the tone; "Excuse me, but" makes it stiff.

**Names and titles.** Use established English names and work titles even when the Hebrew title differs ("מיליארד סיבות לשוד" is Tower Heist; "שבעה צעדים" is Oldboy; "מקום בתוך היער" is The Place Beyond the Pines). Where Nim glosses the Hebrew title himself ("או באנגלית, Tower Heist, שזה יותר 'שוד המגדל'"), keep his aside and let it explain the Hebrew title to an English reader.

**Quotations.** English answers are translations of the published Hebrew. Do not reconstruct what the actor "must have said" in English. Do not polish an interviewee's grammar beyond what the Hebrew shows.

**Captions.** Ynet's telegraphic caption style ("X. Y") can be kept as a short two-part caption in English; it reads naturally as a photo caption.

### 6.3 A worked example of the rhythm (translation of a published passage, W058)

Hebrew: "העלילה, אם אפשר להשתמש במונח בהקשר זה, מספרת על צלם אופנה בינלאומי שמחליט לצלם בישראל. הוא מביא איתו דוגמנים מכל העולם, כולם חתיכים כמובן, והחבורה מצלמת על רקע נופי ישראל. וכמובן, כל סט צילום שכזה נגמר בסקס סוער. שוקינג."

English that keeps the rhythm: "The plot, if the term applies here, follows an international fashion photographer who decides to shoot in Israel. He brings models from all over the world, all of them gorgeous, naturally, and the gang shoots against Israeli scenery. And naturally, every one of those shoots ends in torrid sex. Shocking."

What was protected: the parenthetical doubt about "plot", the two "naturally"s that set up the deadpan, the one-word verdict. What was not added: an adverb, a second joke, an explanation.

## 7. Writing new material on Nim's behalf

The corpus is journalism in Hebrew for Israeli readers between 2005 and 2019. It contains no blog posts, no academic prose, no professional email, and no English originals. The guidance below applies the stable traits; it does not claim to establish how Nim writes in those contexts. When Nim gives explicit instructions for a task, those override everything here.

**Blog and portfolio writing.** Open with an observed detail or a proposition and its undercut. Keep paragraphs short and end them on their strongest clause. One parenthetical aside per paragraph at most. State opinion plainly but frame it as what you saw. End on a small observation or a callback, not a summary. Use only facts, references, and experiences Nim has supplied; never invent an anecdote because the voice "likes anecdotes".

**Personal commentary.** The narrator is a participant who comes off slightly worse than the people around him. Self-mockery is allowed once or twice; it should not become the subject. Draw the moral from the event in one or two sentences.

**Cultural criticism.** Concrete examples named and dated. Judgment explicit, hedged only in delivery ("there's a sense that"). Humor in the examples, not in the thesis. Comparisons should come from Nim's actual frame of reference; do not manufacture pop-culture yardsticks.

**Interview introductions and questions.** Introduce with age, credentials, one detail, one quote. Prompts short; some as statements. Follow-ups reuse the subject's own word. Provocation only where the subject and outlet invite it.

**Professional or academic communication.** Keep: concreteness, short declaratives after a longer sentence, the habit of saying where the document is going before going there, plain explicit conclusions. Drop: mock-honorifics, slang, pop-culture comparisons, flirtation, transliterated English. Light dry humor is acceptable at most once and only if the audience and purpose allow it. The corpus does not establish Nim's register in this context; treat the result as "Nim's structural habits applied to a professional register", not as his professional voice.

**Substance versus style.** Style is the rhythm, the asides, the placement of judgment, the endings. Substance is what is claimed. The profile licenses the former only. Opinions, credentials, memories, quotations, and facts come from Nim or from verified sources, never from the profile.

## 8. Do and don't

**Do**

- Let one observed detail carry the judgment.
- Alternate a long, accumulating sentence with a short verdict.
- Use a parenthesis to correct, confess, gloss, or deflate, about once per hundred words of narration.
- Announce a structural turn in plain words.
- State the opinion; soften only the delivery.
- Put the joke or the judgment in the last clause of the paragraph.
- End on the subject's line or a callback.
- Let a prompt be a statement.
- Follow a sharp line with a concession when the person deserves one.
- Go completely straight when the subject is grief, illness, or harm.

**Don't**

- Don't add a joke the Hebrew does not have, or a second joke after the first lands.
- Don't explain a joke.
- Don't use intensifiers to make understatement "land".
- Don't stack parentheticals or asides in every sentence.
- Don't replace Israeli references with foreign equivalents.
- Don't make the narrator the hero or the victim.
- Don't end with a summary, a lesson about life, or a rhetorical flourish.
- Don't let skepticism harden into contempt.
- Don't import entertainment-column slang into opinion, professional, or academic text.
- Don't invent catchphrases; none of the devices above is a phrase to repeat.

## 9. Ways an AI is likely to misrepresent this voice

1. **Joke inflation.** Adding quips to every sentence because the profile says "humor". The real rate is one or two per paragraph in comic pieces and zero in serious ones.
2. **Snark drift.** Turning "amused skepticism with concessions" into sustained mockery.
3. **Slang overload.** Using the vocabulary list in section 3B as a seasoning. Those words appear across 17,800 words of narration; most sentences contain none of them.
4. **Reference farming.** Inserting Israeli celebrities as comparisons wherever possible. Nim uses them when they fit the thing being described.
5. **Parenthesis stacking.** More than one aside per paragraph starts to read as a tic.
6. **Hero narration.** Making the first-person narrator competent, insightful, and in control. In the corpus he is late, laughed at, scolded, glared at, and sunburned.
7. **Sentimental endings.** Replacing a small observation with a warm moral. Even the vipassana column ends on an observation about the city, not on gratitude.
8. **Generic AI connective tissue.** "Ultimately", "In a world where", "It is worth noting", "This raises the question". None of these occurs. Nim's connectives are plain: "ובכל זאת", "מצד שני", "בכל מקרה", "ונחזור ל".
9. **Hebrew syntax in English.** Verb-first sentences, dropped subjects, and 40-word sentences that were fine in Hebrew. Split them; keep the punchline last.
10. **Flattening the register mix.** Rendering everything in one neutral register loses the collision of formal and street Hebrew that is the most distinctive thing about the prose. In English, keep an occasional formal connective ("as befits", "one notes") next to a plain colloquial verdict.
11. **Over-glossing.** Explaining every reference in a clause turns the prose into a footnote. Gloss only where the joke depends on it.
12. **Treating headlines and subtitles as his.** They may be editorial. Build the voice from body text.

## 10. Revision checklist

For a translation:

- [ ] Every paragraph, prompt, answer, heading, caption, and credit is present and in order.
- [ ] Each prompt is bold and in its own paragraph, including statements.
- [ ] Each paragraph ends on the clause the Hebrew ends on.
- [ ] Long sentences were split only where English required it, and the final clause kept its position.
- [ ] One-word and very short sentences survived as short sentences.
- [ ] Parentheticals are still parentheticals and still carry their function (gloss, confession, deflation).
- [ ] Israeli references are kept, glossed only where needed, never replaced.
- [ ] Transliterated English in the source is rendered as plain English without a compensating invented joke.
- [ ] Mock-honorifics and epithets are preserved with equivalent weight.
- [ ] Understatement was not intensified; concessions after sharp lines were kept.
- [ ] No sentence was added, no joke explained, no conclusion appended.
- [ ] Established English names and titles used; the Hebrew title choice recorded where it differs.
- [ ] Quotations read as translations of the Hebrew, not as reconstructed English speech.
- [ ] Serious passages stayed serious.
- [ ] Read the English alone for fluency; then compare any change back to the Hebrew.

For new writing:

## 12. Annotated demonstrations (illustrative, newly written, not quotations)

The three short passages below were written for this profile to show editorial choices. They are not from any published article and contain no biographical claims.

**A. A brief aside that earns its place.**
Illustrative: "The gallery was on the fifth floor of an ordinary office building (the kind with a dentist on three), and nothing about the lobby suggested what was upstairs."
Why it works: the aside is a single concrete detail that lowers expectations so the next sentence can raise them. It is not a joke on its own.

**B. A skeptical observation framed as impression, then conceded.**
Illustrative: "He answers every question with a story about a famous friend, and after the third one it's hard not to count. Then again, the stories are good, and he tells them well."
Why it works: the judgment is explicit ("hard not to count"), framed as the narrator's experience, and followed by the concession the corpus almost always supplies.

**C. Setup and punchline placement.**
Illustrative: "The menu listed a tasting of seven courses, each with its own wine, its own story from the chef, and its own small ceramic dish that had apparently been made for the occasion. The bread was good."
Why it works: the accumulation is the setup; the verdict is the short last sentence; nothing explains it.

## 13. Copy-ready instruction block for future agents
