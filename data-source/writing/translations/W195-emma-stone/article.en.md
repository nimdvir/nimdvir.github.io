*With two sizzling films on the screens, an upcoming role as Spider-Man's girl and the status of a fashion icon in the making, Emma Stone is shaping up as Hollywood's hottest It Girl at this very second. That still doesn't answer the question that really matters to us: is she a redhead or a blonde?*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in nrg Maariv on August 29, 2011. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/47/ART2/274/272.html).*

Here are a few signs that Emma Stone has turned from just another actress into Hollywood's sizzling It Girl: 1. She is currently starring in two mega-publicized films at once. 2. She will play Spider-Man's girl in the new film in the series, due in theaters soon. 3. Everyone is talking about her style. 4. Her picture appears this month on the cover of the prestigious magazine "Vanity Fair" wearing nothing but a bikini. Does anyone need further proof?

Until a few years ago it looked as if Stone, 23, was going the way of the common trash actress. Nothing at the start of her career suggested she would become a star. Certainly not the sexist comedies she broke out in, "Superbad," "The House Bunny" or "Ghosts of Girlfriends Past," in all of which she played the ditzy hot chick. Nor did her wildly successful horror comedy, "Zombieland," exactly mark her as a promise. After all, she spent most of it covered in blood while slaughtering zombies. And still, even through the clouds of trash it was possible to see that the red-haired Stone (and in certain cases blonde, more on that later) had real charm. Something in her personality, in her toned figure and in her excellent acting ability suggested there was a chance Stone was here to stay.

And indeed, in the past year Stone has been racing at full speed. She has already won the lead in the comedy "Easy A," for which she was nominated for the Golden Globe for best actress, and these days she is scorching the screens in two high-profile films at once: "Friends with Benefits," in which she dumps Justin Timberlake, who consoles himself in the arms of Mila Kunis, and "Crazy, Stupid, Love," in which she plays the love interest of the smoldering Ryan Gosling. Next year, as noted, we will get her in the lead role of Gwen Stacy, Spider-Man's girl in "The Amazing Spider-Man," the new, highly anticipated remake of the successful film series. And if that isn't enough, according to the latest rumors she and Justin Timberlake are set to co-star in a remake of the immortal "Dirty Dancing." Do you really need more than that?

## A career from age 15

Stone, a native of Scottsdale, Arizona, is the daughter of a homemaker mother and a building-contractor father. Her real name is actually Emily Jean, but "Emma" has been her nickname since she was little. She began acting in community theater at 11. She didn't always have self-confidence. "I was an anxious child," she said in an interview. "On the 'Zombieland' shoot we discovered that both I and the actor next to me, Jesse Eisenberg (star of "The Social Network," N.D.), had our first anxiety attack at age eight. That connected us immediately. I had separation anxiety, I constantly worried my house was going to burn down, I played with my hands, and in the end my parents took me to psychological treatment. While I was being treated I understood that acting is my medicine."

Acting really did get her out of it. "When I started studying acting and improv, my anxiety just disappeared completely," she says. "The way children learn teamwork and gain confidence in sports, that's what acting was for me. I learned how not to stutter, how to hold a conversation, how to take risks, and how to get excited even about a glorious failure. Acting taught me a lot, and I understood at a very young age that okay, this is my job."

The result was that Stone gave up on middle school, and chose to spend the time at home and invest in local theater productions. Later her parents sent her to a Catholic girls-only institution, but that lasted one semester. According to the stories (hers), one evening she gathered her parents in the living room and presented them with a PowerPoint presentation set to Madonna's song "Hollywood," in which it was explained to them in detail why young Miss Stone shouldn't be studying but should move to Hollywood and focus on an acting career. "I couldn't study," she says.

"I wanted to act, and it was clear to me that this was my calling. Luckily, my parents supported my dreams and helped me fulfill them."

Following the successful campaign of persuasion, 15-year-old Stone left school for good and moved with her mother to Los Angeles to focus on acting. Although in all her interviews Stone chooses to present herself as the driving force behind her young career, a slight suspicion arises that her parents had a hand in it too. After all, it's no trivial matter for a mother to leave home and move with her daughter to another city in order to turn her into a star.

And so, from age 15, Stone was "homeschooled" (allow us to add: literally), and spent her days at auditions across the city. She doesn't remember this period as a good one, since she absorbed quite a few negative answers in her first days in the City of Angels. "My mom used to drive me places, and she always listened to country music in the car," she recounts. "There's an old Garth Brooks song that says 'some of God's greatest gifts are unanswered prayers.' There are a lot of my prayers that I'm glad went unanswered. In fact, I'm glad I didn't get a lot of roles at 15. I'm not sure I could have handled it then. Now I have a feeling everything is much more right. Really, sometimes the things that don't happen are what makes you who you are."

The road to fame passed, of course, through reality TV. In 2004 Stone took part in the VH1 reality show "In Search of the New Partridge Family," in which actors competed for the right to appear in a remake of the veteran series. Although she snagged the role of Laurie on the show, the remake series itself never got past the pilot stage. Stone describes the moment she auditioned for the role of the cheerleader in "Heroes" as rock bottom, and says she was humiliated in the audition room when the casting people fell at the feet of Hayden Panettiere, who ultimately got the part, and ignored her completely, which made her leave the room in tears. Ouch.

Later she guest-starred on shows like "Medium" and "Malcolm in the Middle," until in 2007 she got a regular role in the Fox series "DRIVE." That year her mother left her, and Stone moved to live on her own in Los Angeles.

With the television work her appetite was whetted, and Stone began steering herself toward feature films. Her string of fairly dumb films, which opened, as noted, with "Superbad," helped brand her as a fairly successful teen-movie actress. The role in "Zombieland" already drew worldwide attention to her, which helped bring her career to where it is now.

## Funny or bitchy?

Stone's sizzling career has already earned her quite a few titles, not all of them in acting. Last April she reached tenth place on the American magazine PEOPLE's list of the "100 Most Beautiful People." For three years running she has been ranked on Maxim magazine's "Hot 100," most recently at number 42. This year she won the award for "Best Comedic Performance" at the MTV awards for "Easy A," and an award for her acting in a romantic comedy at the "Teen Choice Awards."

At the same time, Stone has also begun scorching the red carpets in an array of stunning looks that have turned her into a sort of fashion icon in the making. Her fashion breakthrough came last January, at the Golden Globes, where she stole the show in a peach-orange Calvin Klein dress that drew a great deal of admiration from the critics. Since then she has kept up sophisticated appearances of a true fashionista, in creations by fashion houses such as Lanvin, Bottega Veneta, Tom Ford, Chloé and Giambattista Valli.

Although she is already considered a fashion icon, Stone doesn't see herself as one. "I don't feel like an overly fashionable girl," she said in an interview. "I do know what I like, and that's what I go with. Shopping for me is like choosing a script. A kind of work, professionalism. I'm not one of those girls who walk into a store, try on clothes for hours, deliberate, take, return. No. With me it's either I love it and take it, or not. It's exactly the same with scripts. Either I know by page ten that it's for me, or not. I approach it coolly."

Stone never stops zigzagging between blonde and red hair. Only recently she was photographed for the covers of "Vanity Fair" and "Elle" in sizzling blonde, and already she's a redhead again. Well, let's set the record straight: Stone is actually a natural blonde. When she acted in the comedy "Superbad," the producer Judd Apatow asked her to dye her hair red, and it stuck. She'll go back to blonde for "Spider-Man." "My friends tell me my hair color is tied to the personality I project," she said in an interview. "When I'm a redhead I'm funny, when I'm blonde I'm bitchy. I told them, 'Okay, thanks a lot. I guess I was bitchy my whole childhood.'"

Stone's future currently looks bright: in addition to "Spider-Man" she will also act in "Gangster Squad," a crime film with high expectations, with Sean Penn and Ryan Gosling (again). At the same time she says her dream is actually to produce films, and she is working on producing a comedy of her own, to be directed by Will Gluck (also responsible for "Easy A" and "Friends with Benefits").

So is she on the sure road to the top of Hollywood? According to the buzz on the media sites, we have a huge star in the making on our hands. "I trust the people around me, mainly my manager and also very, very far from me," she says. "I admire people like Woody Harrelson and Steve Carell, whom I worked with, who manage to hold on to both a career and a family. I have a life, but I haven't built my family yet. I feel I'm too scattered, both wanting and not wanting. Maybe my biological clock has already started ticking? Is this the age when it starts? I don't know. It's hard, with all the traveling I do. In my job, you enter a microcosm of people, get close to them, and then after the project ends you part from them and don't see them again. It's as if my life is one summer camp after another, without really going home in between. It keeps me from really forming real relationships."

Although she moves in the right crowd, Stone manages to protect herself from the tabloids' wrath. We haven't yet seen pictures of her drunk or doing the kind of things that sent Lindsay Lohan's career downhill. "I'm lucky that my parents will never let me lose my head like that," she says in the context of Lindsay. "They just won't let it happen. I need that, knowing they'll always be there to look after me. I'd be a wreck without it. I need people by my side I can cry to. I don't have a thick skin, and I don't want to grow one either. I want people beside me who'll protect me. I see a lot of actors in the profession who aren't real, because after all the years of getting hurt they've grown a rhino's skin that keeps them from really connecting. I don't want that to happen to me too."
