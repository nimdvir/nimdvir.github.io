*Is there any reason to avoid flaunting homosexuality on the television screen? Yuval Ganor argues there is not, and responds to Nimrod Dvir's column: "Only when each of us is truly free to be who he is will we be able to say that homophobia has come to an end"*

*Third-party coverage, not Nimrod Dvir's writing. English translation of the Hebrew original by Yuval Ganor, published in Ynet on June 14, 2009. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3730911,00.html).*

When homophobia comes from gay men, it annoys me the most. But it is even more annoying when it comes disguised as principles, or as a "proud" agenda. Last week [Nimrod Dvir called here](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3729243,00.html) on all the gay men who appear on our television screens to stop making a thing of their sexual orientation. Not all of them, just some of them. The others he praised for presenting their gayness "with pleasant ease."

*Pride Parade 2009. A bit of everything (Photo: George Ginsberg)*

And so we got the distinction between Leon Schneidrowski, the "suburban queen," who "rode the homosexual card like a cowgirl in withdrawal," and Erik and Ofir from "Survivor," who are first and foremost a doctor and a lawyer.

Between Amir Fay Guttman, who gave too many interviews about his kiss with his boyfriend, and Gal Uchovsky.

I admit that on first reading I nodded in agreement. I too loved the way Erik made his way to the "Survivor" final and to the win, almost without the question of his sexual orientation being brought to the fore. What's more, when [the news of his victory](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3720238,00.html) appeared on ynet, out of curiosity I searched the 400 talkbacks for how many of them mentioned that he is gay, and was pleased to find few. Truly a worthy representative of the community. But something in that column still nagged at me, and I read it again.

I tried to check whether there really is a difference between Ofir the "solid" one and Amir the one who "makes a thing of it." After all, the reunion between Ofir and his ex-partner was smeared across half an episode of "Survivor," and Assi Azar came out of the closet with a fanfare in "Seven Days." So what was Leon's sin, Leon whom I don't recall talking for more than a minute and a half about his sexual orientation, that he and not Azar earned the title "curiosity"?

*Leon and Bublil on "Big Brother." "Talked for a minute and a half about his orientation" (Photo: Channel 2)*

And I actually remember that Asaf and Gal from "The Block" were presented on the program as "gallery owners," not as "gays." So what, after all, is the difference between them and Dr. Erik Alper?

## Give Us a Doctor, or a Lawyer

The answer is simple. Assi, Ofir and Erik are "straight" gays. They look like straight men, they talk like straight men, and if you bring them to the next family event, no aunt will guess. But if you bring Leon or Gal to that same event, you will most likely become the talk of the week in the family.

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->

*Fay Guttman and his boyfriend Yanai. Bring him home to your parents (Photo: Anat Mosberg)*

What bothered the column's author is not the publicized "flaunting" of sexual orientation, but the sheer flamboyance of those who carry it. Or in other words: not seeing "suburban queens" on screen, that is, feminine and flamboyant gay men. Give him a lawyer, a doctor, or at least a solid media man, "mature and level-headed," and please leave all the rest at home, maybe even in the closet.

Sounds familiar, doesn't it? This is exactly the argument I hear in almost every talk I give to teenagers. Live your lives, but why demonstrate and march with all those feathers and pom-poms in the city center? Or alternatively, a quote from one of the talkbacks written to the column: "Flamboyant gays are simply disgusting." And there you have the entire doctrine of homophobia in five words.

So what can you do, not all gay men are Erik, Ofir and Azar. There are also Leon and Amir and Asaf and Gal and Miki Buganim, some more flamboyant and some less, some more feminine and some less.

By the way, to my taste Leon, Asaf and Gal brought their gayness into the reality shows "with pleasant ease" at least as much as Erik and Ofir did. But only when each of us is truly free to be who he is, without suppressing any part of his personality, and only when within the community itself everyone accepts everyone, but truly accepts, will we be able to say that homophobia has come to an end.

The author is the director of the radio station 88FM and a volunteer with [Hoshen](http://www.hoshen.org/), the education center of the gay, lesbian, bisexual and transgender community in Israel.
