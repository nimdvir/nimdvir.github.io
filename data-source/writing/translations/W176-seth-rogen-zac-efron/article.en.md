*Zac Efron was the youngest, hottest teen star in Hollywood, until the grim statistics caught up with him. Then Seth Rogen came along with an offer to star in the hysterically funny Neighbors, and though the shoot had its share of anxiety and incidents, these days he keeps to a healthy routine and looks ahead with optimism. And why not? He's Zac Efron*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Ynet on May 15, 2014. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4518968,00.html).*

One of the fun parts of interviewing **Zac Efron** is trying to keep count of the nonsense he sends out into the room. He opens, for instance, by insisting that he knows me from somewhere (I nearly melted in total belief, except that I had watched him do the exact same bit for a journalist from Portugal a few minutes earlier). He also mentions that he almost flew to Israel for a visit last week, but something came up and wrecked the plans (again, the Portuguese reporter got a similar story).

Efron, 27, generally seems to have no trouble bending the truth. Take his insistence that the bit at the [latest MTV Movie Awards](http://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4510041,00.html), where **Rita Ora** ripped his shirt off on stage, was pure improvisation. "I swear I had no idea it was coming," he laughs. "Nothing was planned. It took me a while to work out what the hell had just happened." Interesting. The body he happened to expose up there, well trimmed and generously slathered in bronzer, tells a different story, and anyway, if the thing wasn't planned, Efron absolutely ought to sue Ora for sexual harassment.

*Sure, honey, it's genetics! Efron gets some personal attention from Rita Ora (Photo: AFP)*

**But let's leave Zacky alone**, since the point of all his verbal juggling seems to be nothing more than getting everyone to like him, one of the better-known Hollywood traits. He makes sure to shake the hand of everyone who comes in to interview him, smiles with a blinding flash of teeth and flirts nonstop through the conversation, even when the human version of Godzilla is sitting across from him. Maybe especially then. It's a sharp contrast with the approach of **Seth Rogen**, the brilliant actor and filmmaker who is his co-star in Neighbors (the reason we're all here), and who seems to have given up long ago on the energy it takes to make people like you, one of the better-known traits of the professional stoner. Rogen, 32, shows up to the interview in sweats that look like pajamas, flirts with absolutely nobody and comes down fairly hard on anyone who dares ask him a dumb question.

Both are Jewish (Efron by way of roots; Rogen is the full article, with parents who met on a kibbutz), both are wildly successful at the moment, and both make an odd pairing given where they come from in film, which is also exactly what sets them so sharply apart. The first is a hunk who broke out in the hunk role on Disney's mega-hit High School Musical and, until very recently (he's now trying to break the mold with a run of daring parts), was known mainly for his impressive hair and an array of abs. The second is a very successful comic actor, a senior member of one of Hollywood's most respected cliques (the one around the writer-director Judd Apatow, who directed Rogen in Knocked Up), and in recent years a successful filmmaker in his own right alongside his writing partner **Evan Goldberg**; the two are responsible for hits like Pineapple Express, Superbad and This Is the End. That contrast is exactly the fuel that drives Neighbors, a comic war between a young couple plus one (Rogen and **Rose Byrne**, who pretty much steals the show as his scheming wife) and the rowdy fraternity that moves in next door, led by Efron and **Dave Franco** (brother of).

"There are a few stories going on here," Rogen says. "Living next to kids is a nightmare, no question, but the film isn't just about the tension between the frat and the family. It's also about a couple dealing with a baby coming into their lives. There's a scene, for instance, where my character has to milk Rose's character. It sounds insane, but it turns out that actually happens. All these stories come our way and we go, hmm, that could make a good movie. The absurdity is grounded in reality. The personal relationships and the reality balance out the million dick jokes. Each of us brings something real to the film. Rose's character, by the way, is based on my wife. Couples in comedies tend not to like each other. There's less emphasis on the relationship. We wanted it to be real, for them to come across as a couple in love."

**What does Zac bring to the film?**

"Blue eyes and a six-pack?" he laughs. "Kidding. He is unusually good-looking, true, but what I liked most is that there's something scary about him. He brings something dark to the character; he's not just a nice guy. Zac makes the frat guys likable, you root for him, and then at some point you start to feel there's something pretty crazy in there. Nothing is scarier than a cheerful guy saying 'I'm going to kill you.' It's so much darker coming from someone who looks as innocent as Zac. We knew he could deliver that. It was obvious he had more to offer. He has the right tools, he just hadn't been given enough chances to use them."

**You said in an interview that before you met him you were sure you wouldn't like him.**

"Absolutely! I knew him as that guy from High School Musical. It was only when we met and started working that I realized Zac is a serious actor. He had all of us riveted."

**For Efron**, landing on a comedy set and working alongside Rogen was no less nerve-racking. "There's something stressful about being surrounded by quick comedians," Efron says. "Comedy is very hard. Someone like Seth makes it look effortless, and it really isn't."

**Cast members say they saw you punching a wall in frustration between takes.**

"I wouldn't go that far. It didn't come easy, but I never got to the wall-punching stage. I just improvised less. I stuck very close to the script. I'm still a student, still learning. I'm trying a lot of things. I want to show a lot of sides."

**Speaking of showing sides, you're shirtless for most of the film. Aren't you worried people won't take you seriously?**

"It's not a serious film, and we didn't take ourselves seriously. It was the safest environment there is; you couldn't fail. The chemistry was so good that we kept adding shirtless scenes. We overdid it. We felt like we were being encouraged to go wild. Every shooting day on this film was a 14-hour party."

*One more and that's it. Efron in Neighbors (Publicity)*

It looks that way, too. Neighbors is soaked in party scenes, heavy drinking and drug use, all of which connect directly to Efron's own circumstances. He recently [gave a long interview to The Hollywood Reporter](http://pplus.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4515506,00.html) in which he came clean about the rumors and half-reports of his exploits over the past few years. These included the fight Efron waged over the past year against his various addictions (the Daily Mail reported that Efron snorted enormous quantities of cocaine, took ecstasy and MDMA, and was addicted to alcohol and to OxyContin painkillers), two stints in rehab for drugs and alcohol, the first in April 2013, and a few especially bizarre incidents, like the one in which he broke his jaw slipping at home and the one in which he and a friend ended up in a brawl with a homeless man in the middle of the night on the streets of Los Angeles. "He had a knife. The police came and broke it up, but it was one of the scariest moments of my life," he said. After the first stay in rehab everything seemed to be heading the right way, but then, during the Neighbors shoot, perhaps because of the stress and anxiety of facing such a gifted comic crew, came another relapse, and Efron had to miss several days of filming to get his head straight again.

**"It's a never-ending struggle,"** he declared in that interview, though he went on to say that today, at least, he has the upper hand. With a steady, quiet routine of workouts, healthy food and bed by 9 p.m., where he reads a script, watches a movie and goes to sleep, Efron is starting to take back the reins of his life. "By and large, he's doing a good job of it," Rogen said of him. "He was a child actor, and you don't need a sociology degree to see the pits waiting for those guys along the way." "There was a time and a place in my life for partying," Efron says. "But my party days are over. This movie was the icing on the cake. We partied plenty on the shoot, and that's me done. I can't anymore. I'm done, big time. These days I'm in bed by ten. Trying, at least." "It's a story about a transitional period, and I'm in it too right now," Rogen announces. "We're evolving from party animals into grown-ups. I don't have a family yet, but obviously the older you get, the more responsibility you have. For me it's actually nice. I'm fine with not partying hard. We're in a process, we're changing. Life changes in general, and you have to be ready for it."

**Would you like kids of your own?**

"I see friends who really enjoy it. For me it's not in the plans right now, no, but I can see it does people good."

**Mac Radner, your character, is a man of the new, more sensitive breed. Is there a trend of men like that in your comedies and the Apatow gang's?**

"I don't think so. If anything, the trend right now is that **Lisa McCarthy** is in most of the comedies (laughs). What I liked about ours is that the woman is just as irresponsible and gets dragged into the stupidity like the boys. To me that's maybe even a little groundbreaking, which is a shame."

**Zac, what are you like as a neighbor in real life?**

"I'm a fantastic neighbor. I share a place with my brother in Los Angeles. If you peeked through my window, I'd probably be on the couch watching a movie. **Charlie Day** from It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia is my neighbor. I'm always peeking at him over the fence. So maybe I'm actually a terrible neighbor?"

**And the fans don't make a racket on the street for the rest of the neighbors?**

"Not really. It's more in public places. It's fun meeting them, I love it, but it's very embarrassing, I have to admit. It's flattering and amazing that people follow me, but I enjoy what little anonymity I have. Like when I go to a movie and have to take pictures with people in the popcorn line. I'm not always in the mood for that."
