*Emma Watson is no longer just Hermione Granger, the nerdy witch. At 21 she is a fashion icon, a designer and now the face of Lancôme. She talks about the short haircut and life after Harry Potter*

In the ten years since the first film in the Harry Potter saga, Emma Watson has gone from Hermione Granger, the annoying girl from the Hogwarts school of witchcraft, the nerd who stops time so she can make it to every class, to a fashion icon who picks the right trend at the right moment. The eighth film, which wraps up the series, opens soon, and Watson will be free of that chapter of her career for good. In the meantime she has already signed a Lancôme campaign and shot two films meant to wipe out any memory of Hermione.

She arrived at [the premiere of the previous Harry Potter](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/55/ART2/178/790.html) in a twenties-style Rafael Lopez dress and cropped hair, a dizzying declaration of independence, adulthood and, above all, freedom from a role that had her tied down. She picks her inspirations wisely: in interviews she names Cate Blanchett and Helen Mirren as her models, two strong actresses who never traded an acting career for celebrity.

The campaigns she has chosen so far, Burberry and now Lancôme, position her high and position her right, well clear of Daniel Radcliffe's leftovers. "I don't feel like I'm leaving Hermione behind, because she's such a big part of me," she says. "I grew up playing her, and we share so many traits that I feel I'm taking a lot of her with me. But I'll definitely miss her life. It was fun living it."

**Are you comfortable in front of the camera?**

"I started acting when I was nine or ten. When you're that young you're barely aware of fear. I was lucky, and it came naturally. I don't remember much of my life from before I was famous."

## Cool Britannia

Thanks to the Harry Potter series, Watson is considered one of the most popular, and richest, young women in the kingdom. About three years ago, when she was 18, there was talk of her joining Chanel's roster of faces. In the end she went with Burberry, a brand that tries to project a traditional-but-cool Britishness and had previously been fronted by stylish Brits like Kate Moss and Agyness Deyn. Watson fit the category like the check on a trench coat.

Meanwhile Watson started standing out on red carpets too, and over the past two years she has become a fashion icon with a style that is original, distinctive, sophisticated, daring and on trend. She also designed a clothing line for the brand People Tree.

**Do you see yourself becoming a designer?**

"I really enjoyed the experience. There are parts of the fashion industry that can be cruel to the environment and to the people who work in it. It's good to get people thinking about what they buy, but I don't think it's something I'll do in the future."

## A Very Loyal Person

A few months ago Watson surprised her fans and chopped off her locks. "I played Hermione for ten years, so I needed that change. And I've always loved short hair," she explains. The Twiggy look she adopted turned out to be a smart decision: over the past year other fashionable stars, Michelle Williams and Carey Mulligan among them, have decided to shear their hair too. In return, every one of them landed covers at the serious fashion magazines.

*Emma Watson on the cover of Vogue, July 2011 (cover photo)*

The campaign for Trésor Midnight Rose, the perfume, which reaches Israel sometime in 2012, was shot by Mario Testino, who also took Kate Middleton and Prince William's engagement portrait and will, by all accounts, shoot Kate Moss's wedding. He has already photographed Watson for the cover of British Vogue, and now for the July issue of the American one.

"Mario is someone I trust a hundred percent," Watson says. "He shot my first British Vogue cover, after all, and I worked with him on Burberry too. I feel he really gets me, as a person, and we have a great working relationship. I think his pictures come out so amazing because he's a warm person with an incredible sense of humor. The result is always beautiful, because the people he photographs are enjoying themselves with him. You just feel happy around Mario. He's hilarious, silly, funny and warm. He never gets stressed."

**How did you decide to join Lancôme?**

"First of all, it was just incredibly flattering. I'm much younger and much less established than the brand's other ambassadors. Lancôme is represented by some amazing women, Julia Roberts, Kate Winslet, Penélope Cruz, women I really admire. They have so much style and integrity, and they're hugely successful. In short, it was just a huge compliment. I don't know why they came to me, but I suppose it might be because I'm five or ten years younger than everyone else they work with, and maybe they wanted someone younger. They look for people who'll represent them for a long time, and I'm a very loyal person. I think we'll get along brilliantly."

**Do you remember your first perfume?**

"Yes. My father took the family on holiday to the south of France, and we visited a perfumer. In the end he encouraged me to pick out a scent, and I remember my stepmother being annoyed because she thought I was too young to have a perfume of my own. I was seven, but I still have the little bottle. I've tried to recreate the scent and never managed."

**Are you a beauty-products girl?**

"Yes! I have tons of products. I've always loved makeup and the way it can turn you into someone else. I don't wear much myself, so it's a little ironic that I have so many products at home."
