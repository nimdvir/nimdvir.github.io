*What I Talk About When I Talk About Running by Haruki Murakami is a gripping diary of devotion, love and the relentless pursuit of a goal. Who would have believed there's so much to find in a book about running*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in nrg Maariv on August 26, 2010. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/47/ART2/149/265.html).*

**"Pain is inevitable, suffering is a choice"**

The worn-out insight above echoed in my head as I read Haruki Murakami's new book, What I Talk About When I Talk About Running (and yes, he uses this cliché too).

Murakami, a renowned Japanese author who has given the literary world Norwegian Wood, After Dark and many more fine works, departs here from his dramatic style and turns to a semi-autobiographical path. His new book, whose title is borrowed from Raymond Carver's What We Talk About When We Talk About Love, is in fact a personal diary in which Murakami details his relationship with an athletic hobby that has accompanied him throughout his life: long-distance running.

A whole book about running sounds like a fairly boring idea. But, as the book proves, when you love something (and borrowing the title from Carver's book proves that this is indeed love) you can write about it with enough feeling, passion and interest to fill 185 packed, gripping pages.

I am no runner, and in fact I maintain the image of a serious couch potato. And still, while reading I could not help sinking with Murakami into the joy of runners, the thrill of waking up in the morning and heading out to exercise, and above all into what for me is the main subject the book deals with: the ability to set a goal and then strive with full strength and energy to achieve it.

Murakami aspires to run the New York Marathon, 42 grueling kilometers through the bridges and avenues of the American city. This is in effect the book's frame story, although there is of course no real plot here.

On the way to achieving his goal, Murakami tells us about his training, his thoughts while running, the techniques he developed to survive the long courses, his credo when it comes to sport, and the mental and physical difficulties he encountered along the way.

Over the course of the book there is a fair amount of life philosophy, which even the reader who is not a runner can connect with. Murakami's style is restrained and informative, and yet very readable and interesting. Einat Cooper's translation from the Japanese succeeds in conveying the author's flowing thoughts, as he tells us a story without pretension, without any attempt to preach and without any particular pride. Simply a book about running.

I can't say the book stirred in me a desire to lace up my shoes and go circle the neighborhood a few times. It did leave me with a feeling of ease and joy, and above all great appreciation for the author and for people like him in general, who do what they love and learn along the way about restraint, patience, devotion and the relentless pursuit of a goal, whatever it may be.

**What I Talk About When I Talk About Running, Haruki Murakami. Translated from the Japanese by Einat Cooper, Keter, 185 pages**
