*Raja Ram, a mega-DJ about to turn 70, arrives next weekend to perform at the Blu4Dance festival in Eilat. An exclusive interview about age and the trick of it*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in nrg Maariv on June 23, 2010. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/47/ART2/123/378.html).*

Ronald Rothfield, otherwise known as DJ Raja Ram, has been in the business for close to 40 years. As he gets ready to celebrate his 70th birthday, Ram isn't exactly resting, and keeps playing around the world as one half of the trance duo Shpongle, alongside Simon Posford. Next weekend he comes to us solo, this time for the Blu4Dance festival in Eilat.

"I've probably been to Israel 20 or 30 times," he says in a phone call from his home base in England. "I'm old, I've had time to get there. I got my first job in Israel in 1999 or 1998, at the Forum club in Beersheba. Since then I keep coming back. It's like my second home.

"I've been all over Israel. It's one of my favorite countries in the world. I love the attitude. The people are friendly, everyone laughs, sits at tables outside and drinks coffee. It's a country with quality of life and calm. I'm Jewish myself, so I feel at home. It really is one of the great countries in the world."

**What can we expect from the set you'll play this time?**

"That's a hard question. With me it's always unpredictable, always a surprise. I'd be happy to make magic with the people. It all comes from the crowd. It's a celebration and we'll celebrate together, with old friends and new ones. It's a wonderful invitation to come to Eilat and play, and I'm looking forward to it. I expect a frenzy of the senses, a feeling of happiness, beauty and purity. That's what we hope for."

**What's it like playing parties at 70?**

"As I got older I thought it would become impossible, that there would be a gap between me and the people partying. But I have to say I don't feel a gap. I've been doing it for 40 years, and it's a blessing from God that I still enjoy it. I can't explain why, because it's beyond logic. It's a great position to be in, traveling the world and playing for people. It's a privilege.

"Music is a drug. It keeps you young and interested in the world. It gives you enormous energy. The moment the party ends I'm off to Chile. I travel huge distances and it really is tiring, but there's something in the air that makes you want to do it and lets you. You dance, you sweat, you play. It's like playing football: there's a lot of pressure on you, but it's enormous fun."

**You know it's hot here.**

"Of course. I know what to wear: sandals, shorts and a T-shirt. I've also got my own fan."
