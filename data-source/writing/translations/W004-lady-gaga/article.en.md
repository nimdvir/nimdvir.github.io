*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Ynet on August 12, 2009. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3760766,00.html).*

The story of Lady Gaga, the most talked-about pop star in the world at this very second, is a story of faith and arrogance. Gaga, or by her full name Stefani Joanne Angelina Germanotta, always knew she was going to be a star. Or more precisely, a kind of global persona named "Lady Gaga," a colorful, moonstruck, spiritual, scribbled-together character she drew with her own hands and released into the tabloid air.

Since she was a girl she fed on the pop culture around her and dreamed of the day she would conquer the scene. And it worked for her: today she sings about paparazzi, fame, celebs and relationships, covers herself in diamonds and bizarre fashion, and markets a notion of New York glamour.

The world, for its part, embraces her: her 3 debut singles reached number one on the American chart, her debut album, "The Fame," sold more than 4 million copies worldwide, she was nominated for the Grammy, she leads the list of nominees for the MTV music awards with nine nominations (beating Britney Spears and Eminem and tying with Beyoncé), she was recently shot for the cover of "Rolling Stone" by the superstar photographer David LaChapelle (who declared her his new muse), and she has already managed to work with the Pussycat Dolls, Kanye West, Akon and more.

At present she's in the middle of a world tour called "The Fame Ball Tour," which is taking her to almost every country on the globe. She'll come to us too, at the peak of her career, as part of the Pelephone company's Non Stop Musix music festival, and will perform on August 19 at the Tel Aviv Exhibition Grounds alongside bands like Kaiser Chiefs and Simple Plan.

These days she's in Tokyo, where she's driving the Japanese crazy with hairdos and an array of bizarre wigs (her hair was purple for a moment, and at one point she appeared with some kind of dead animal on her head) and with interviews even more extreme than that (in which she confessed to her fondness for monsters and to her mystical ability to foresee what goes on in the pop world).

## Five years back at New York University

In honor of her arrival in Israel we were granted a phone call from the Lady, straight from Tokyo. I come to the interview with her a little excited: apart from the fact that I love her very much ("Poker Face" is, as far as I'm concerned, the song of the year), we also have a point in common on the résumé: I knew Gaga, or Stefani, back in the days when we shared a school bench, five years ago at New York University.

Although she studied music and I studied other things, I knew of her existence because of Gaga, a character who was active back then too. In those days she was a kind of party promoter and local personality, who organized nights at well-regarded clubs and performed in downtown Manhattan. Those were the days when she created the alter ego called Gaga, colorful, glittering, sexually blurred, and strutted around the city with it. And then she started to take off. Today, in exactly the clubs she used to promote, they play her songs.

After an excited Japanese operator connects us, I remind her of our shared period. "Wow," she laughs, "that was four years ago, something like that. I've changed radically since then. I left the university and a lot has happened since. I was 19 then, making music, studying music, wearing underwear. A different time."

**If I remember correctly, that was a period when you were deep in the party scene. You also did quite a few drugs, and even talked about it in interviews.**

"Yes. There were a lot of drugs then. In those days I'd hang around at parties doing all sorts of things that today I choose not to do. That was a dark period. Now I'm in the light."

Before we go on, we have to explain how Stefani Germanotta turned from a party girl into a global phenomenon. She was born 23 years ago in New York to fairly well-off parents of Italian descent. Her father is an internet entrepreneur, her mother works in telecommunications.

Throughout her childhood she studied music privately (including playing the piano) and went to a prestigious Catholic school with the sisters Paris and Nicky Hilton. When the time came for university, she enrolled at New York University to study music, but her head was elsewhere.

Even then the idea of Lady Gaga was starting to cook, and at 19 she signed a contract that didn't last with Def Jam Records. She didn't despair and began performing in clubs around the city, promoted parties and took part in all sorts of burlesque shows, in one of which she stood in a swimsuit and blew soap bubbles over the crowd (later she would explain that it was "art, and anyone who doesn't get it doesn't know what he's talking about").

## "Even my family started calling me Gaga"

In that period she teamed up with a local drag queen called Lady Starlight and the two became good friends. Starlight helped Gaga consolidate her style and brought her closer to the gay community. The two's joint show became the talk of the town, and Gaga, with her colorful personality, began to build herself a name. At 20 she left school and signed a contract with Interscope Records, at first in the capacity of songwriter.

*The most interesting thing the pop world has produced in a long while (Gettyimages Imagebank)*

After becoming the protégée of the singer Akon, she started working on her debut album, "The Fame," which came out in 2008. The album became a dizzying success and produced a string of hits that reached the top of the charts: "Poker Face," "Paparazzi," "Just Dance" and more. Gaga was nominated for the Grammy, collaborated with the Pussycat Dolls on their tour and gradually became a global phenomenon.

"Call me Gaga," she asks before we continue the interview. "Back then too, when we studied together, they called me Gaga. It's been that way for years. All my friends call me Gaga. Even my family started calling me that, but that's more as a joke."

**But Gaga is a kind of fictional character, isn't she?**

"No. Gaga is a work of art, but she's completely real. Gaga is me. There's no difference."

Gaga sees herself as an artist in every fiber of her being. Even the sentences she releases into the air are wrapped in a semi-sophisticated aura, usually accompanied by a pause that smells of self-importance. She has a New York/Andy Warhol style of speech (which you can read about at length on p. 56), in which every sentence of hers is delivered slowly, out of a sense that she has just handed the world the next verbal Mona Lisa. Her show, too, she describes several times as "a pop show for museums." Leonardo da Vintage.

"I always knew I was going to be a star," she says between one pause and the next. "I knew it was my destiny, to tell stories. Lady Gaga is a story. What I do is tell a story to the audience, to the world. I always knew I'd do this. I also knew I'd make music. I always wanted to live life in an artistic, creative way. I'm very glad I got lucky and succeeded."

**You've succeeded big time. The last two years have been madness for you.**

"No. The madness is here and now. The madness is today, it's tomorrow. In the last few months the madness around me has been at its greatest. I have three singles at number one. I'm nominated for the MTV awards. The madness is stronger than ever. I feel it and I like it."

**Congratulations on the MTV awards. You're nominated for nine awards, the most nominations of any artist, an honor you share with Beyoncé.**

"Thank you. It's exciting. It's all thanks to the good people I've worked with."

**So what's the secret of such success?**

"There's no secret. I work hard. I have a good house of artists. I think the secret is that I collaborate with good people. That's why I succeed."

## "It was a great honor to perform with the Pet Shop Boys"

Speaking of good people, a lot has been written about Lady Gaga in fact being no small fraud, with the secret of her success lying in super-professional production. That is, the producers are the ones who give the sting to songs like "Poker Face," while in live performances Gaga turns out to be a mediocre-minus singer. It was enough to watch her performance at the last Brit Awards, where she sang a tribute song with the Pet Shop Boys and was as off-key as a run-over cat. When I ask her about it, she says, "I was very moved at that moment. It was a great honor for me to perform with the Pet Shop Boys."

**Are you aware of the criticism that "Gaga" is more production than talent?**

"That's really not true. I write all my songs. I compose them. I work with the producers. I am Gaga. That doesn't contradict the fact that there are good people with me who help me in the process."

The songs Gaga wrote, at least those that appear on the album, mostly talk about fame, the love of it and the longing for it, and the difficulty of being famous. A little funny, since they were written long before she was famous herself.

"I talk about glamour, you don't have to live it for that," she says. "I talk about a glamorous life even without real glamour and cameras around. My songs are about arrogance. You don't need to be famous to understand them. Arrogance is a good thing. All my work is about image, about a lie that in the end becomes the truth. I want my fans to feel famous, to feel like stars. I'm telling a story about a dream, about New York, about glamour you create on your own."

**Speaking of New York, do you miss the city on all your travels around the world?**

"I miss the city and I work in it too. I'm everywhere. I come back to New York sometimes, but right now my focus is on the work. I do what I do for the kids, my kids at home."

**Which kids are you talking about?**

"The kids are my friends. All the ones who support me."

The full term is "club kids," New York slang describing the party-and-glamour people, the ones who accompanied Gaga on her meteoric rise to the top. But even though she supposedly does what she does for them, not all of them are generous about it. For instance, on my last visit to New York I had the chance to go out to a party with those very kids, the gang that accompanied her in her university days and in the city's party and performance life.

At a certain moment "Poker Face," the song that launched her upward, was playing in the background. "You know what the difference is between us and her?" asked Steven, a failed actor who used to hang around with her, pointing at his friends. "That she's up there," and he pointed at the fearsome speakers her voice was blasting from, "and all of us stayed down here." That bitterness apparently characterizes Gaga's friends from the New York scene, who feel a little abandoned behind.

**Do you feel that your friends from the party days support you? You've essentially fulfilled their dream.**

"Everyone has a story to tell," she says. "I'm telling my story right now."

**So now all your friends are famous?**

"I don't have famous friends. Maybe one or two. I don't choose people by how famous they are."

## "It took my father a long time to accept what I do"

Gaga may not surround herself with celebrities, but she has enough media preoccupation of her own. For instance, right now the hot topic in the entertainment press is the preoccupation with rumors about her sexual identity, with the latest buzz being a report that Gaga is in fact a man.

To back up the claim, a YouTube clip was circulated around the web showing a segment from a show in which she lifts her skirt and supposedly reveals a bulge in her underwear. The website Bossip.com immediately published an "interview" with her, fabricated in all likelihood, in which she admits that she's a hermaphrodite, meaning she has both female and male genitalia, and says she loves her penis.

Her personal manager rushed to issue a vigorous denial, and the lawsuit is apparently already on its way. When she's asked about the subject, Gaga prefers not to comment. "I don't talk about myself or about my personal life," she says. "All those rumors are ridiculous in my eyes, and I prefer not to address them at all."

**You're always very provocative. I remember when you were just starting out, you'd go out to parties nearly naked. You once said in an interview that your parents refused to talk to you in that period.**

"Yes, it was new and different for them. They didn't understand that it was art. It took my father a long time to accept what I do. He understands now. Today he sees me as an artist, he understands that what I do is create. Not just be provocative."

**And that creation takes you far.**

"Right," she laughs, "thanks to my work I'm in a different country every day. Today I'm in Japan, tomorrow in South Korea, then the Philippines, then China. Music is a powerful thing. It takes me around the world. Thanks to my work I travel the world, meet the fans, write music, design. I live a life full of passion."

**And full of money.**

"Not necessarily. Now, when I make money, I invest all of it in my art. In the shows, in the costumes, in the videos. I didn't buy myself an apartment. I don't waste my money on nonsense like clothes, apartments and life's pleasures. I don't have a home of my own, I have nothing. Everything goes back into the event that is Lady Gaga. I love investing in design. For instance, the bubble piano I use in the show I designed myself. I'm a full partner in the design of my video clips."
