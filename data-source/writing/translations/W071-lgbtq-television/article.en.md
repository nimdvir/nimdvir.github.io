*We have no problem with the gays taking over our prime time, but waving the "gay" label around on television can easily turn into a cheap gimmick. Nimrod Dvir thinks it's time to stop making a thing of it*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Ynet on June 10, 2009. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.ynet.co.il/article/3729243).*

Drop someone from the nineties into Israel 2009 and he won't believe the pink running riot on the screen. Arik and Ofir are showing their pride on the beaches of [Survivor: Pearl Islands](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3720321,00.html), Gal Uchovsky and Dana International are chattering away on [A Star Is Born](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3698084,00.html), Aviad Kisos is camping it up on Basic Instinct and Wai at Ten, Amir Fay Guttman and Corinne Allal are planting smooches on their partners on Big Brother, and Leon Schneiderovsky is giving fine representation to the suburban-queen genre on the most-watched show in the country.

*Fay-Guttman and the boyfriend. A kiss in prime time? What's the big deal? (Photo: Eliran Avital)*

Honestly, no complaints about quantity. Since Iggy and Tomer's steamy kiss on the late, lamented [Florentine](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DaurjL6GAlo), it seems as if television is gradually turning into a branch of the Aguda, the national LGBT association.

We've had Rami and Ronen on Ratzim LaDira, Yonatan Koniak with his assortment of characters, the trashy Pick Up, Assi Azar flirting with guys on Good Evening with Guy Pines, Shorty talking about her love of vaginas on My Blog, not to mention the fine import from abroad, Queer as Folk, which at the start of the decade taught an entire generation what anal sex is.

In short, as with clothing stores and El Al planes, the community has managed to squeeze its way into television with a respectable presence. And still, even after a decade-plus loaded with television representation, there are cases where homosexuality is a trait synonymous with deviance and difference.

## What's the Issue?

Presence on the screen is our calling card, the way people see the community. It seems that this year too, after we've been through quite a few gays in prime time, the label "gay" is still a gimmick, something you bring on to show the uncles from Rishon LeZion the different and the deviant.

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->

*Asaf and Gal. The main thing is that there are gays on The Block (Photo: Eldad Rafaeli)*

Take The Block, a supposedly enlightened reality show, in which the rather lovely couple Asaf and Gal are presented in the role of "gays," and heaven forbid not by their professions. Note too the community's own tendency to sin by self-flaunting and thereby cause its own segregation: Leon, cute as he is, rode the homosexual card like a cowgirl in withdrawal, and turned "gay" into a gimmick stuck to his name. Amir Fay Guttman, too, after leaving the house, gave interviews about the kiss with his boyfriend and made the matter into an event on the scale of a papal visit.

Now, if we're into acceptance, why force yourself out of the mainstream? Being gay is exactly like being a member of any other minority group. You can wear it on your lapel, or you can simply be yourself.

## Yes to Pride, No to Flaunting

As far as I'm concerned, it's precisely the ones who don't try hard who are making the real change on television. Arik and Ofir, who are first of all a doctor and a lawyer, brought the matter of sexual orientation into Survivor with a pleasant lightness.

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->

*Arik and Ofir. Some people don't make a thing of it (Photo: Anat Mosberg)*

Assi Azar, who no longer talks about his boyfriend all the time, has become a household name, and Gal Uchovsky's cool approach on A Star Is Born gives representation to more mature and measured voices. All of the above have proved that you can be gay and still an equal among equals.

I'm not saying anyone should hide their orientation or be ashamed of it. Quite the opposite. Everyone should follow their heart, and the television screen should be open to everyone, with all their orientations, cultures, mannerisms and agendas. The problem starts when we turn ourselves into a curiosity.

If gays can take the million, host the most-watched show in the country or judge alongside Margol, there is no reason "gay" should be the main title that leads them on their way.
