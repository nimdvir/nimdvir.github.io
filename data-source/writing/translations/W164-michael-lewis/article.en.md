*Michael Lewis got tired of being called a draft dodger, so he packed up his abs and left Israel. He got out of Survivor VIP even faster (blame the heart problems), in the hope of making it in New York. If he doesn't survive there either, he can always say yes to the job offers he keeps getting from the porn scene*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Xnet on May 5, 2012. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://xnet.ynet.co.il/xo/articles/0,7340,L-3094833,00.html).*

Preparing to meet Michael Lewis made me feel like Demi Moore trying to seduce a young boy toy. After a session of hesitation on his side, and massive, obsessive persuasion on mine, I managed to drag Lewis to my place at 11 p.m., with both of us already a few exotic drinks in.

While I was lighting scented candles and preparing for the art of seduction, Michael walked into the living room wearing a faded hoodie and short sports shorts with tights underneath, which is apparently something athletes do. Not that it needs saying, but the man is a hysterical hunk. And the surprise, from my point of view: he's also fairly intelligent and charming.

Lewis may be taking part in Survivor VIP, but here in New York he isn't exactly living the VIP life yet: he lives in the not-so-good part of Harlem, in an apartment with three roommates ("there isn't a single white person on the street"). He arrives at our meeting sweaty after a workday in which he gave a personal training session and did a shift as a bartender.

"I've been in the United States on and off for five years now," he says. "I moved here officially three and a half months ago, when I got my work visa. Luckily I have a big résumé, so I didn't have much trouble getting it."

There's a sense that Lewis, only 24, has always been around. He broke through at 15 as a model with provocative interviews, and from there kept climbing the Israeli entertainment industry, leaning on his handsome looks and on the abs that long ago became an Israeli heritage site.

Lewis fronted the Fox campaign, appeared in HaAlufa, Danny Hollywood, and The Pilots' Wives, did children's series and the Festigal, and even won the reality show Dancing with the Stars. Despite all that, he moved to New York with a suitcase full of anger at what was done to him in Israel.

"The people of Israel, for me, are the reactions I get when I walk down the street there," he says. "In Israel I'd get more hateful reactions than supportive ones. People are angry at me for not doing the army, they shout that I'm a draft dodger and stuff like that. I've explained it many times before: there's a real reason I wasn't drafted. I have heart problems, and the army doesn't deal with people in my condition. I wanted to volunteer, I filled out forms, they didn't get back to me, so I carried on with my career.

"My reason is real, not fake like some other people's, without naming names. Why dump it on me? In my opinion it's because I look good and made a lot of money in that period. They opened the pile-on on me after Dancing with the Stars, along the lines of 'how can you do the show and not the army?' After I won, everywhere I went people shouted 'draft dodger,' 'go do the army,' things like that."

**There's something to it. On the one hand you're unfit for the army, and on the other you're prancing around on Dancing with the Stars, about to appear on Survivor, and you shot Wipeout too.**

"I don't give a fuck. I know there'll be reactions, good and bad, and there's nothing to be done. It also comes from the media, which presents me in whatever light it wants.

"Survivor was a childhood dream of mine. I remember watching the first American season and saying, 'wow, I have to do that.' Yes, I knew there'd be reactions like 'how can he do Survivor if he can't do the army,' but at the end of the day what matters to me is to do what I love and not give a shit about anyone.

"I'm an athlete at heart, and every sport I've done in my life was on my own responsibility. The army didn't want to take responsibility for my condition, so I moved on. Channel 10 agreed to take responsibility, so I went. What, I'm not going to do something because it bothers people?

"People are bothered that I'm tall and have abs. If I were fat and short, nobody would care about me. Nothing to be done. People in our country don't like successful people. The moment someone succeeds they step on you, pull you down. It's a problem in our culture. I felt it in Israel every day. Did you know they sprayed giant graffiti across from my house, 'Michael Lewis's mother, get cancer and burn'? They also sprayed graffiti right on my house: 'Michael Lewis, you son of a bitch.'"

**There's a chance Survivor will make it worse.**

"I take my cue from Americans on this: be yourself and don't give a damn about anyone else. Here people succeed because they stay themselves. I grew up on that mentality. I'll try to move forward in my profession in the most straight and honest way possible, but I won't account for what others think. They didn't draft me, so I do what's good for me in life. I'm not hurting anyone. All in all I think I'm an okay guy. I'm not a shit, I'm not a jerk."

Already from the promos for the VIP season of Survivor you could see that something not good was happening to Michael in the first episode. The item that ran in the papers at the time under the headline "Didn't survive two weeks" pretty much revealed that Lewis's condition on the island wasn't brilliant.

"I overdid it," Michael says. "I had no food, I had no water. At some point the body does a restart."

**Funny that yours restarted and Buki Naeh's didn't.**

"I was in the frenzy of my life. By the first day I understood this wasn't going to be easy. Everything in the game is very real, and pretty quickly you can see that my medical problem is real. I think that regardless, I would have been among the first out, because I was a very big threat to everyone else. On Survivor the underdogs always take it. I wasn't an underdog."

Throughout the interview Lewis is fairly tired, the result of a packed shift at the bar where he works, Antib. The rest of the time he runs between modeling and acting auditions, courtesy of the agencies he's signed with in the city, Innovative Artists and Request Models.

"I have to work," he says. "I'm a hyperactive person, I have to keep myself busy. So I train at the gym and do two bar shifts a week. It's to stay focused. In this city you get lost very quickly, especially when you don't have an agenda. So I sleep five hours a night and work all day. It's not for the money, it's for my sanity."

Lewis has a girlfriend, Jess, a stunning 24-year-old model (I've seen pictures). "She should be a doctor but she wants to be a model," he says. "She's a knockout. Every time I see her I stop breathing." They met at the modeling agency and have been together a year and a half. She's a Christian of Mexican descent who lives in a Catholic neighborhood on Long Island. "I have no problem with it," he says. "My previous girlfriend was Muslim. So what?"

**With your looks, aren't they trying to pull you into the porn scene?**

"I've had so many offers, you have no idea. I've gone to so many auditions where suddenly, regardless of why I came, they asked me if I'd be willing to do porn. But I always say no. I watch a lot of porn. I'm a big fan of porn and I have no problem with it, but I would never do it. Not for me."

**For all your achievements, you give the impression that you left Israel not at a high point but at a low one.**

"I left during a low period, but that's life. You can't stop at a red light, you have to keep going. My feelings about Israel are that I want to come back. That's where I want to be. I love speaking Hebrew, I'm very much a Zionist, and here I feel like I'm in the diaspora. But right now I want to be here, because I think I have the potential to make it here in the entertainment industry."

**And if it doesn't happen? The competition is tougher than in Israel.**

"If it doesn't work out, I'll find something else. But at least we tried."
