*Michael Lewis got tired of being called a draft dodger, so he packed up his six-pack and left Israel. He got out of Survivor VIP even faster (blame the heart condition), hoping to make it in New York. And if he doesn't survive there either, he can always say yes to the job offers from the porn scene*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Xnet on May 5, 2012. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://xnet.ynet.co.il/xo/articles/0,7340,L-3094833,00.html).*

Setting up a meeting with Michael Lewis made me feel like Demi Moore trying to seduce a young lover. After a round of cold feet on his side and massive, obsessive persuasion on mine, I managed to drag Lewis to my apartment at 11 p.m., both of us already a few exotic drinks in.

While I was lighting scented candles and preparing for the art of seduction, Michael walked into the living room in a faded hoodie and running shorts over tights, which is apparently something athletes do. Not that it needs saying, but the man is ridiculously good-looking. And the surprise, as far as I was concerned: he's also intelligent and really quite charming.

Lewis may be on Survivor VIP, the Israeli celebrity edition of Survivor, but here in New York the VIP life hasn't quite started: he lives in the wrong part of Harlem, in an apartment with three roommates ("there isn't one white person on the street"). He shows up to our meeting sweaty after a workday of personal training sessions and a bartending shift.

"I've been in the States on and off for five years," he says. "I moved here officially three and a half months ago, when I got my work visa. Luckily I've got a big résumé, so it wasn't much of a problem getting it."

Lewis is only 24, but it feels like he has always been around. He broke through at 15 as a model giving provocative interviews, and from there climbed the Israeli entertainment industry on the strength of his looks and a set of abs that long ago became an Israeli heritage site.

Lewis fronted the campaign for the fashion chain Fox, acted in the Israeli series The Champion, Danny Hollywood and The Pilots' Wives, did children's shows and the Festigal, Israel's annual kids' holiday spectacular, and even won the Israeli Dancing with the Stars. Despite all that, he moved to New York with a suitcase full of anger at what was done to him in Israel.

"For me, the people of Israel are the reactions I get walking down the street there," he says. "In Israel I'd get more hate than love. People are angry that I didn't do the army, they shout that I'm a draft dodger, that kind of thing. I've explained it plenty of times before: there's a real reason I wasn't drafted. I have a heart condition, and the army doesn't deal with people in my situation. I wanted to volunteer, I filled out the forms, nobody got back to me, so I got on with my career.

"My reason is real, not fake like some other people's, no names. So why pin it on me? I think it's because I look good and was making a lot of money at the time. The pile-on started after Dancing with the Stars, along the lines of 'how can you do the show and not the army?' After I won, everywhere I went people shouted 'draft dodger,' 'go do the army,' stuff like that."

**They have a point. On one hand you're unfit for the army, and on the other you're prancing around on Dancing with the Stars, about to be on Survivor, and you shot Wipeout too.**

"I don't give a fuck. I know there'll be reactions, good and bad, and there's nothing to be done about it. Some of it comes from the media, which shows me in whatever light it likes.

"Survivor was a childhood dream. I remember watching the first American season and saying, 'Wow, I have to do that.' Yes, I knew there'd be the 'how can he do Survivor if he can't do the army' reactions, but at the end of the day what matters to me is doing what I love and not giving a shit about anyone.

"I'm an athlete at heart, and every sport I've ever done was at my own risk. The army didn't want to take responsibility for my condition, so I moved on. Channel 10 agreed to take responsibility, so I went. What, I'm not going to do something because it bugs people?

"It bugs people that I'm tall and have a six-pack. If I were short and fat, nobody would care about me. Nothing to be done. People in our country don't like successful people. The minute someone makes it, they step on you, they pull you down. It's a problem in our culture. I felt it every day in Israel. Did you know somebody sprayed giant graffiti across from my house, 'Michael Lewis's mother, get cancer and burn'? They sprayed graffiti right on the house too: 'Michael Lewis, you son of a bitch.'"

**Survivor might make it worse.**

"I take my cue from Americans on this: be yourself and don't give a damn about anyone else. Here people succeed because they stay themselves. I grew up on that mentality. I'll try to get ahead in my profession in the straightest, most decent way I can, but I'm not going to keep score of what other people think. They didn't draft me, so I do what's good for me. I'm not hurting anyone. All in all I think I'm an okay guy. I'm not a piece of shit, I'm not an asshole."

You could tell from the promos for the VIP season of Survivor that something was going wrong for Michael in the first episode. The item that ran in the papers at the time under the headline "Didn't Survive Two Weeks" pretty much gave away that Lewis's condition on the island wasn't great.

"I overdid it," Michael says. "I had no food, I had no water. At some point the body just reboots."

**Funny that yours rebooted and veteran broadcaster Buki Naeh's didn't.**

"I was in the craziest state of my life. By day one I understood this wasn't going to be easy. Everything in the game is very real, and pretty quickly you see that my medical problem is real. I think that regardless, I'd have been one of the first out, because I was a huge threat to everyone else. On Survivor the underdogs always win. I was no underdog."

Lewis is fairly worn out through the interview, the result of a packed shift at Antib, the bar where he works. The rest of the time he runs between modeling and acting auditions, courtesy of the agencies that signed him in the city, Innovative Artists and Request Models.

"I have to work," he says. "I'm hyperactive, I have to keep myself busy. So I train people at the gym and do two bar shifts a week. It keeps me focused. In this city you get lost very fast, especially when you don't have a plan. So I sleep five hours a night and work all day. It's not for the money, it's for my sanity."

Lewis has a girlfriend, Jess, a stunning 24-year-old model (I've seen pictures). "She should be a doctor, but she wants to be a model," he says. "She's a knockout. Every time I see her I stop breathing." They met at the modeling agency and have been together a year and a half. She's a Christian of Mexican descent who lives in a Catholic neighborhood on Long Island. "I have no problem with it," he says. "My last girlfriend was Muslim. So what?"

**With your looks, doesn't anyone try to pull you into porn?**

"I've had so many offers, you have no idea. I've been to so many auditions where suddenly, whatever I'd come in for, they asked if I'd be willing to do porn. But I always say no. I watch a ton of porn. I'm a big fan of porn and I have no problem with it, but I'd never do it. Not for me."

**For all you've achieved, you give the impression you left Israel at a low point, not a high one.**

"I left during a low, but that's life. You can't stop at the red light, you have to keep going. My feelings about Israel are that I want to go back. That's where I want to be. I love speaking Hebrew, I'm very Zionist, and here I feel like I'm in exile. But right now I want to be here, because I think I have the potential to make it in the entertainment industry here."

**And if it doesn't happen? The competition is tougher than in Israel.**

"If it doesn't work out, I'll find something else. But at least we tried."
