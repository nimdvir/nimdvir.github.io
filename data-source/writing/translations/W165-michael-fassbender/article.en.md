*Michael Fassbender's third film with director Steve McQueen may yet win him an Oscar. In 12 Years a Slave he plays a plantation owner who, in keeping with the times, brutalizes his slaves. In an interview he says he hopes all that is behind us, and that his villain is too: "It would have been pretty perverse of me to use this as therapy"*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Ynet on January 20, 2014. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4478514,00.html).*

For a lot of people, watching 12 Years a Slave is a harrowing experience. Steve McQueen's new film tells the true story of Solomon Northup (Chiwetel Ejiofor), kidnapped and sold into slavery in the American South of 1841, and it is so violent and so emotionally dense that it takes a while to come back to ordinary life after you leave the theater.

If it is hard to watch, it was worth wondering what it did to Michael Fassbender. McQueen's go-to leading man (he was in both of the director's earlier films, Hunger and Shame) plays Edwin Epps here, a cotton planter with a taste for tormenting his slaves. "Honestly, by the end of the shoot what I mostly felt was relief," he says. "I was glad we'd finished, and I hoped we'd come out with something worth keeping. The way Steve works, you leave everything on set at the end of the day. By the time I got home I was already working on whatever was next."

Fassbender, 36, German-born and raised in Ireland, is known to audiences as the young Magneto in Matthew Vaughn's X-Men: First Class, and from Zack Snyder's 300 and Quentin Tarantino's Inglourious Basterds. Most of his fame, though, he owes to the partnership with McQueen. "Steve changed my life. I owe him everything," he admits. "We hit on a very good chemistry very fast. I get a huge amount of inspiration from him, and I hope I give some back."

**What makes him different from the other directors you've worked with?**

"He's very demanding, but he's also very reassuring, and he really lets actors follow their instincts. He makes it a very safe place to fail. He encourages the idea of trying and failing, then trying again and failing better. Actors usually have tricks we lean on, things that worked before, so we do them again. Steve has a big bullshit detector. He forces you to drop your guard and keep it real."

*Steve McQueen and Michael Fassbender. Quite the pair (Photo: Gettyimages)*

Speaking of dropping your guard: after dropping the weight for Hunger and going full frontal in Shame, came 12 Years a Slave. Fassbender seems to take it a step further here, as a violent villain, violent toward women above all, who abuses his workers on the sweltering farmland of Louisiana. The role, incidentally, has earned him an [Oscar nomination for Best Supporting Actor](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4477534,00.html).

"It's an intense role, but I made a point of keeping my distance from it," he says. "It would have been pretty perverse of me to use this character as some kind of therapy. It's an important story, so I went into it with a lot of respect and a lot of responsibility. I wanted to take on the responsibility of becoming Master Epps because I think he's so central to Solomon's journey. More than anything I wanted to honor Solomon and all the slaves. You could practically feel their spirits dancing around the plantations."

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Michael Fassbender as Edwin Epps in 12 Years a Slave*

**You handle the whip like an expert.**

"I had to train for it. We're talking about a whip nearly two meters long. There's a technique to it, because it can come back at you. I practiced the crack a lot. The same way Chiwetel practiced the violin, I practiced the whip."

**For all he does, there's something miserable about Epps too.**

"I wanted to give him a human element, so we could recognize some spark of ourselves in him and then go home and say, hang on, maybe we should be treating each other better? Solomon's story is horrifying, of course, but the people holding the whips were damaged too, by what it did to their souls. My wife in the film (Sarah Paulson, who is wonderful) ends up a very pitiful figure as well. Their marriage is falling apart, they have no children, and her husband is cheating on her. She's human, and she's reacting to her insecurity and her fears.

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Michael Fassbender in 12 Years a Slave. A complicated character*

"Epps is the same. He's just reacting to his fears. He's in love with Patsey, one of his slaves, and he has no idea what to do with that, so he sets out to destroy her. It was a complicated world. Slavery isn't only the relationship between master and slave. It's also the story of the country's economy. You see Solomon go from hauling freight to picking cotton to a sugar plantation. The film shows you how much that society's economy depended on slavery."

**Do you think slavery, and the way people treat each other in this film, could come back today?**

"I hope humanity has changed since then. History has shown that human beings can do powerful things for each other, and the opposite too. Look at all the sweatshops today where children make the clothes everyone in the West wears. That's a kind of slavery, I suppose. But I hope we learn to work together for good."

*Michael Fassbender. Life in the fast lane (Photo: MCT)*

When he isn't beating slaves in Louisiana, Fassbender divides his time between London and Los Angeles, travels whenever he can, and goes in for extreme sports, racing above all. "I love riding motorcycles, I'm learning to surf, I love traveling, new cultures, new people," he says. "Usually the minute I wrap a shoot I'm gone, already on my way to some adventure. I've always been athletic and physical, very active. I love karting and racing, I love watching motorsport. Karting is as close as I'll ever get to Formula 1, though my dream is to write, direct and star in a film about car racing."

**What about a personal life? Marriage, say, or a family?**

"I feel like that would be having my cake and eating it too. I have a wonderful job, the one I always wanted. I suppose I've had to sacrifice things along the way. I don't know what's around the corner. I might meet someone and fall in love, but most of my attention goes to the work. I spent a lot of years waiting to work, and now I finally get to do it. I think creative people have a period when they do their best work, and the years before and after are a kind of run-up or a decline. I'm in my period now, and I want to focus on it."

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Michael Fassbender, Steve McQueen and Chiwetel Ejiofor. An Oscar on the way? (Photo: GettyImages)*

**You're also up for an Oscar for this role. More proof you're in a good period.**

"I couldn't ask to be in a better position. When I started acting at 17 or 18, where I am today was the dream.

"I'm working with the best people in the business now, and I have a wonderful partnership with Steve. He brings out the best in me. I think Steve did a wonderful job here and made a beautiful film that has an incredible effect on people. To me that matters more than any award."

**So is there a chance of a fourth film together?**

"I really hope so. I love working with him. As far as I'm concerned, if Steve picks up the phone, I'm in. I said yes to 12 Years a Slave before I'd read the script. Steve just told me he was making a film about slavery and I said of course, straight away. It's essential for me to be part of projects like this, alongside the more entertaining ones."
