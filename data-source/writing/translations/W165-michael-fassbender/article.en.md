*Michael Fassbender's third collaboration with director Steve McQueen may yet bring him an Oscar. In 12 Years a Slave the actor plays a plantation owner who, in the spirit of the times, brutalizes his slaves. In an interview he expresses hope that the phenomenon is behind us, and so is his villain: "It would have been pretty perverse of me to use this as therapy"*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in Ynet on January 20, 2014. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4478514,00.html).*

For many people, watching 12 Years a Slave is a shattering experience. Steve McQueen's new film, which tells the true story of Solomon Northup (played by Chiwetel Ejiofor), kidnapped and sold into slavery in the American South of 1841, is so violent and so emotionally concentrated that it takes a while to return to everyday life after leaving the theater.

If it's hard for the audience to watch, it was interesting to think about what Michael Fassbender went through. McQueen's in-house actor (he worked with him on his two previous films, Hunger and Shame), he plays Edwin Epps here, a cotton plantation owner with a taste for abusing slaves. "Honestly, by the end of the shoot what I mostly felt was relief," he says. "I was glad we'd finished, and I hoped we'd made something worth keeping. The way Steve works, at the end of the day you leave everything on set. By the time I got home I was already working on whatever was coming next."

*The trailer for 12 Years a Slave*

Fassbender, 36, born in Germany and raised in Ireland, is known to audiences for his roles as the young Magneto in Matthew Vaughn's X-Men: First Class, and in films like Zack Snyder's 300 and Quentin Tarantino's Inglourious Basterds. Still, he owes most of his fame to the collaboration with McQueen. "Steve changed my life. I owe him everything," he admits. "We developed a very good chemistry very quickly. I get a huge amount of inspiration from him, and I hope I give some back."

**What sets him apart from other directors you've worked with?**

"He's very demanding, but he's also very reassuring, and he really lets actors follow their instincts. He creates a very safe place to fail and encourages the idea of trying and failing, and then trying again and failing better. Actors usually have tricks we lean on, things that worked in the past, so we do them again. Steve has a big bullshit detector. He forces you to drop your defenses and keep it real."

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Steve McQueen and Michael Fassbender. Quite the pair (Photo: Gettyimages)*

Speaking of dropping defenses: after losing weight for Hunger and exposing his genitals in Shame, came his turn in 12 Years a Slave. Fassbender seems to go a step further here, in the role of a violent villain, violent toward women above all, who abuses his workers on the sweaty farmland of Louisiana. For this role, incidentally, he is [nominated for the Best Supporting Actor Oscar](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4477534,00.html).

"It's an intense role, but I made a point of keeping my distance from it," he says. "It would have been pretty perverse of me to use this character as some kind of therapy. This is an important story, so I went into the character with a great deal of respect and a great deal of responsibility. I wanted to take on the responsibility of becoming Master Epps because I think he's so significant in Solomon's journey. More than anything I wanted to honor Solomon and all the slaves, whose spirits you could practically feel dancing around the plantations."

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Michael Fassbender as Edwin Epps in 12 Years a Slave*

**You show real expertise with the whip.**

"I had to train for that. We're talking about a whip almost two meters long. There's a technique to working with it, because it can come back at you. I practiced the lash a lot. Just as Chiwetel practiced playing the violin, I practiced using the whip."

**Despite what he does, there's a certain misery in Epps too.**

"I wanted to put a human element into him so we could recognize some spark of ourselves in him, and then go home and say, hold on, maybe we should treat each other a little better? Of course Solomon's story is horrifying, but the people who did the whipping were damaged too, by what it did to their souls. My wife's character in the film (wonderfully played by Sarah Paulson) is, in the end, very pitiful. Their marriage is falling apart, they have no children, and her husband is cheating on her. She's human, and she's reacting to her insecurity and her fears.

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Michael Fassbender in 12 Years a Slave. A complicated character*

"Epps is the same, just reacting to his fears. He's in love with Patsey, one of his slave women, and he doesn't know how to deal with that, so he sets out to destroy her. It was a complex world. Slavery isn't only the relationship between master and slave. It's also the story of the country's economy. You see Solomon go from hauling to picking cotton to a sugar farm. The film shows how important slavery was to that society's economy."

**Do you think slavery, and the kind of treatment of human beings shown in the film, could return in our day?**

"I hope humanity has changed since then. History has proved that human beings are capable of doing powerful things for one another, and also the opposite. After all, these days there are plenty of sweatshops where children make the clothes that all of us in the West wear. That's a kind of slavery, I suppose. But I hope we learn to work together for good ends."

*Michael Fassbender. Life in the fast lane (Photo: MCT)*

When he isn't beating slaves in Louisiana, Fassbender splits his life between London and Los Angeles and tries to travel the world and take part in extreme sports, especially racing, which he loves most of all. "I like riding motorcycles, I'm learning to surf, I love traveling, experiencing new cultures, new people," he says. "Usually the minute I finish shooting, I'm gone, already on my way to some adventure. I've always been athletic and physical, very active. I really love karting and racing, I love watching motorsport. Karting is the closest I'll ever get to Formula 1, although my dream is to write, direct, and star in a film about car racing."

**What about a personal life? Say, getting married or starting a family?**

"I feel like that's having your cake and eating it too. I have a wonderful job that I always wanted to do. I suppose I've had to sacrifice things along the way. I don't know what's around the corner, I might meet someone and fall in love, but most of my attention goes to the work. I spent so many years waiting to work, and now I finally have the chance to do it. I think that for creative people there's a period when each of us produces our best work, and the years before and after are a kind of preparation or a decline from it. I'm in my creative period now, and I want to focus on that."

<!-- Source image not embedded; see source-record.json for provenance and licensing. -->
*Michael Fassbender, Steve McQueen and Chiwetel Ejiofor. An Oscar on the way? (Photo: GettyImages)*

**You're also nominated for an Oscar for your role here, more proof that you're in a good period.**

"I couldn't ask to be in a better position. When I started acting at 17 or 18, where I am today was my dream.

"These days I work with the best people in the business, and I have a wonderful partnership with Steve. He brings out the good things in me. I think Steve did wonderful work here and made a beautiful film with an amazing effect on people. To me that matters more than any award."

**So is there a chance we'll see a fourth film from the two of you?**

"I very much hope so. I love working with him. As far as I'm concerned, if Steve picks up the phone, I say yes immediately. I said yes to 12 Years a Slave before I'd even read the script. Steve just told me he was making a film about slavery and I said of course right away. It's essential for me to be involved in projects like this, alongside more entertaining ones."
