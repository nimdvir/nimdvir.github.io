*The distance humanity has traveled, from walking barefoot in a forest clearing to flying to the moon, and from ignorance to the glut of information offered on the internet, has not changed the basic picture in which humankind appears inferior and defeated before the forces of nature*

The companion report is by Shlomo Tsezana, Nitzchia Yaakov and Dan Lavie, with Meital Yasur Beit-Or, as credited in the source. Nimrod Dvir is credited only as the photographer of the lead image. *Photo: Nimrod Dvir // The road to Thorong La (Nimrod Dvir)*

The distance humanity has traveled, from walking barefoot in a forest clearing to flying to the moon, and from ignorance to the glut of information offered on the internet, has not changed the basic picture in which humankind appears inferior and defeated before the forces of nature, which go by many names. Those inclined to religious faith speak of man's nothingness before God, in the spirit of King David's psalm: "What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him?"

In the secular essence of disaster we are speaking of wind and storm and earthquake and volcanic eruption and landslide and tidal surge and floods and fire. To those close to the center of the disaster they usually look like the end of days. Why did Lot's daughters lie with their drunken father and bring into the world their descendants, the impure Moab and Ammon? Because they believed that if they did not sin in this way, the human race would come to its end after the punishment God had inflicted on Sodom and Gomorrah.

What moved Amos to deliver his prophecy precisely "two years after the earthquake"? What did the residents of Pompeii think in the year 79, as they watched their city, a treasure in its own right, erased and scorched beneath the waves of boiling lava spewed from Mount Vesuvius? Only over the course of human history did man learn that these disasters, whose source lies in hidden higher powers, are not final. In an avalanche in the Alps 98 years ago tens of thousands of people were killed at once, but the First World War, bloodier than its predecessors, went on shedding blood without pause. People understood that disaster does not stop the wheel of human life.

The media has accelerated two processes connected with natural disasters. It has heightened awareness and sped up the flow of information, and it has also established the feeling that within a short time the world will return to normal, for better and for worse, until the brief agitation at the time of the next disaster. So too with the tragedy of the avalanche in the Himalayas in Nepal, in which hundreds of people are involved, among them many Israelis.

Throughout the holiday, everyone with a relative or acquaintance or friend there, or anyone indirectly counted as such, was on edge, waiting for a scrap of information about the fate of their loved ones. Thoughts also raced, mixed with anxiety: had the avalanche only come a week earlier or later, and skipped before or after the Sukkot holiday, it is likely that fewer Israelis would have been in the area. Who will cry Job's cry to the heavens to ask why precisely on the eve of Simchat Torah? And who will answer?

But this is the essence of Western, modern life in the 21st century. Young people want to experience; to be a little Christopher Columbus or Vasco da Gama; they rush to the ends of the earth; the price is not high in Western terms, even in a country where Milky is not cheap; and transport to the foot of the sought-after sites is convenient.

"This tune cannot be stopped," as the Air Force song goes. Nor is there any point in trying. No young person will give up driving because of the accidents, nor the post-army trip to scenic sites, and what has been is what will be, and we will be on edge, and we will mourn, deeply, as in these days, and the young will carry on in the spirit of "the world goes on as usual."

## Deadly Avalanche

**A severe storm struck the Annapurna range in the Himalayas about a month earlier than forecast • The result was disastrous: at least 29 trekkers from various countries were killed, among them 3 Israelis; fears for the life of another trekker**

Disaster at the height of trekking season: on Tuesday, at around 06:00 Nepal time, a severe storm struck the popular Annapurna range, about a month earlier than forecast. At least 29 backpackers lost their lives in an avalanche caused by the storm, among them three Israelis. As of press time, another Israeli is listed as missing and there are fears for his life. So far 17 injured Israelis have been rescued and taken for treatment.

According to estimates, the avalanche resulted from the remnants of Cyclone Hudhud, which struck southeastern India this week. The avalanche hit the Thorong La mountain pass in the Annapurna range as early as Tuesday evening, but the Nepalese army's rescue teams had difficulty reaching the site because of harsh weather conditions and the depth of the snow that had piled up there, which according to reports reached two meters. Only on Wednesday morning, as the weather cleared, were the rescue teams able to help evacuate the casualties.

Below the range, local army personnel set up a camp to receive those who have been and will be rescued, and they are being transferred to Kathmandu and Pokhara for treatment. Local media reported that about 70 people are still missing and more than 153 trekkers have already been rescued. An official in the Nepalese government estimated yesterday that it would take several days to recover all the bodies.

The agency responsible for trekking in Nepal was forced to admit that this is "the most serious incident involving foreign nationals to occur in the area in the past decade." In addition, the association of tourism agencies in Nepal sharply criticized the local authorities: "The government is happy to take money from tourists but does nothing for them. Now it needs to make sure it funds weather forecasts and rapid assistance to rescue trekkers."

Despite the holiday being observed in Israel, the director general of the Foreign Ministry, Nissim Ben-Sheetrit, held a special discussion at his ministry in which all the parties handling the event in Israel and Nepal took part. According to him, the department for Israelis abroad, the Foreign Ministry's situation room and the Israeli embassy in Nepal are operating in emergency mode to handle the event.

In the situation assessment Ben-Sheetrit said that "128 young people who were there have been located and we are in contact with them; contact has not been made with 135 more people. That does not mean they are in danger, but that there are communication problems there. Most of them are in a safe place. I assume we will be able to locate most of these people by tomorrow morning."

According to him, the forces on the ground are trying to rescue those present and complete the rescue within two days, because after that another wave of stormy weather is expected. "There is no need for a rescue delegation. The Nepalese are helping and know how to do this better than anyone, and they have provided the maximum assistance for the purpose," he added.

According to the head of the ministry's situation room, Ilana Ravid, the Israelis who are on the mountain and have not yet made contact are classified as "out of contact" rather than missing, and according to the available information they are not in danger. From the information Ravid provided, it emerges that the Foreign Ministry has compiled the names of 200 Israelis who are in the area, with whom contact has been made and who have been confirmed not to be in danger.

Ravid also said that the Nepalese authorities are cooperating fully in passing information to Israel's representatives on the ground: Ambassador Yaron Mayer and his deputy Yehonatan Lebel. The Foreign Ministry decided to reinforce the mission in Nepal with a team from India. Already yesterday at noon the search and rescue teams of Magnus Search and Rescue set out, as did those of the Harel insurance company, "Harel 669." In a situation assessment held by Magen David Adom director general Eli Bin, it was decided to prepare to dispatch a delegation of an MDA doctor and paramedic.
