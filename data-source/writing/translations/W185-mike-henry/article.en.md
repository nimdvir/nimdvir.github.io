*The actor Mike Henry, who happens to be white, is the man behind Cleveland, the star of the animated series The Cleveland Show. In an exclusive interview he explains how his show differs from Family Guy, talks about working with Kanye West, and describes what it's like to voice an African American character*

*English translation of the Hebrew original by Nimrod Dvir, published in nrg Maariv on March 30, 2011. [Read the original in Hebrew](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/nrg/online/47/ART2/227/227.html).*

"It's very cool to be interviewed for an Israeli paper," says Mike Henry, 47, the actor and voice artist behind Cleveland Brown, star of the hit animated series The Cleveland Show (Saturdays at 6:50 p.m. on Israel's Yes Comedy channel). "The Cleveland Show is written mostly by Jews, so this is very exciting."

*I'm not a racist person. Mike Henry (publicity photo)*

The show, now in its third season in the United States (and its second in Israel), is a spin-off of the hit Family Guy. It follows Cleveland Brown, an African American starting a new life with his son and a new partner. "When the decision came down to make a Family Guy spin-off, I told myself it was about time. I was thrilled," Henry says. "Early on it was clear to me that Cleveland had a lot more to offer than what you saw on Family Guy. To my mind he was the perfect pick for a spin-off. We didn't want to make another Family Guy. We wanted something that would feel different."

**What's the difference?**

"We're nicer. The Cleveland Show isn't as mean as Family Guy. Yes, we make fun of celebrities and the humor gets wild, but basically the show is aimed at families. It's a sweet show with a less crazy vibe."

**How does a white man end up behind a Black character and a Black show?**

"I created the character while we were working on Family Guy, so naturally I voice him too. I do a lot of characters: Black, white, women, perverts, everything. I'm not a racist person, so I don't worry about someone criticizing me. It goes the other way too. We have an actor in the cast, Kevin Michael Richardson, who is Black, and he plays Cleveland's son and voices white characters as well. We don't worry about it. As long as whoever's doing the voice is funny, I don't care whether he's Black or white. I think animation is freer that way."

*A sweet show. The Cleveland Show (publicity photo)*

**One of the high points was Kanye West's guest spot on the show.**

"Right. We heard Kanye was a fan of the show and asked him to come in. He said yes right away. He came twice and took a real interest in the whole process. On our show he made fun of South Park after they'd made fun of him. He was very professional."

**Cleveland travels the world over the course of the show. How about a quick trip to Israel?**

"That could be fun. I'm sure we'll get there at some point. We're in the middle of the third season and we've been to Yemen, Hawaii and a few other places. Maybe down the line we'll make it to Israel."

**How do you split the work with Seth MacFarlane, the creator of Family Guy?**

"The show is my responsibility, and Seth and I oversee every script. I direct the voice acting and I have creative say on every episode. You have to take the network's notes into account, of course, and most of the time they're pretty reasonable. Seth is busy with Family Guy and leaves The Cleveland Show in our hands. We make the show we want to make. He doesn't have time to get into it."

**Are you planning to stay in animation?**

"I love animation because you can go anywhere with it. You can get away with a lot because it's animation. On Family Guy, for instance, I voice a pedophile. In animation you can present serious things as farce, and nobody takes us too seriously. But down the road I want to act in other things too. You could say I 'fell' into animation. I was making 'regular' films until I met Seth and joined up. In the future I definitely want to act in real films."
