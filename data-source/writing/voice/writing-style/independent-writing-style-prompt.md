# Prompt for an independent Hebrew and English writing-style report

Analyze Nimrod Dvir’s writing from the original source texts. Produce an evidence-based, bilingual report that another writer or editor can use to draft and revise in his voice. The aim is to reproduce his choices about what to notice, what to say, and how to say it, down to sentence rhythm and paragraph movement. This is the first, independent pass. Save your findings for a later comparison that Nim will initiate.

## Source and independence

- Work from `data-source/writing/originals/` in the `nimdvir.github.io` repository. Inventory the full folder recursively. Use `article.he.md` as the reading copy when the package verifies it. Consult `source-record.json` for provenance and use the clean HTML or archived PDF to resolve meaningful differences in wording, completeness, or layout. Treat copies of the same article as one work. Use `data-source/writing/articles.csv` only for source metadata such as byline, language, dates, and duplicate links. Keep the original files read-only.
- Leave existing research under `data-source/writing/voice/`, translation drafts, before-and-after comparisons, translator briefs, and previous AI analyses unread. Use only the archive and the permitted inventory metadata for evidence. Complete and save this report before any discussion of agreement, disagreement, or merging with other reports.
- Check author, original composition language, completeness, and duplicate status per work. Use a title, tag, or `styleSample` value as a clue and verify it against the source. Track partial and blocked packages, uncertain bylines, coauthored work, and third-party coverage. Where authorship is mixed, use only clearly attributable passages as direct evidence. Exclude unsupported attributions from author-level claims and explain the exclusion.
- Distinguish Nim’s narration, transitions, framing, and attributable interview questions from interviewee speech and other quoted material. A quote can show his choice of placement or framing even when its words belong to someone else. Treat headlines, decks, intertitles, and captions as potentially edited and label them accordingly. Treat indications of extraction or OCR errors as source issues. Use genre, outlet, period, or editorial conventions as competing explanations where the evidence allows.

## How to analyze the corpus

1. **Account for coverage.** Record which distinct works you read in full, which you read in part, and which you could only inventory. Record the reason for each partial read or exclusion. Group the usable work by language, genre, outlet, and period. Read every eligible article from opening to ending. Work in batches if needed and keep per-article evidence notes as you go so that the final synthesis represents the full corpus. Report actual coverage and explicitly mark any gaps.
2. **Read at three levels.** At the article level, track openings, narrative stance, information order, how context enters, the movement between scene and explanation, interview sequencing, and endings. At the paragraph level, track topic turns, pacing, juxtaposition, transitions between voice and quotations, and the setups and payoffs of humor. At the sentence and word level, track register shifts, syntax, rhythm, sentence length, clause structure, punctuation, recurring expressions, specificity, point of view, and how directness and restraint vary. Where relevant, examine how Nim treats the people he writes about, frames disagreement, expresses uncertainty, and prepares or follows up on interview questions.
3. **Check distribution and alternatives.** For recurring traits, look across unrelated articles and across genres, outlets, or periods when the corpus supports it. Give denominators and scope for frequencies or measurements and explain how you counted. For manual coding, define what qualifies and note how much you coded. For automated counts, describe language-specific token or sentence boundaries and which kinds of text were excluded. Check whether a single long article, interviewee, publication convention, or archive artifact drives the apparent pattern. Record cases where Nim chooses differently and what appears to prompt the change. Mark distinctive one-off moves as examples, with a separate status from recurring habits.
4. **Test the draft guide.** Use metadata to set aside several representative articles across available genres before close reading. Record the first set of rules before opening those articles. Read them in full, record rules that hold, need narrower scope, or fail, and explain revisions. Give a short editorial demonstration per language. Use an independently written, clearly labeled same-facts before-and-after example to show what the guide changes and why. Keep the examples separate from corpus evidence. Preserve facts, uncertainty, and attribution in each rewrite.

## Handle Hebrew and English on their own terms

- **עברית.** Write the practical Hebrew guide in natural Hebrew. Show where Nim uses conversational and formal registers, specific word choices, syntactic order, punctuation, sound, or rhythm. Use short original Hebrew passages with their article references. Explain what a choice does in the passage and when a writer would make it again. Ground Hebrew usage rules in attributable Hebrew prose.
- **English.** Check the allowed sources for prose verifiably composed in English by Nim. Report the evidence base, even if it is empty. If verified originals exist, analyze them directly and give observed English patterns their own evidence. Label any translator-written, AI-generated, editorially rewritten, or uncertain versions if encountered within the permitted sources and keep them out of the native English evidence. An English title or an inventory claim of an English version establishes a lead only until the body and provenance are verified within the allowed corpus.
- **Hebrew-to-English transfer.** Give an English guide even if direct English evidence is sparse or absent. Identify which choices the Hebrew evidence supports transferring and how to realize them in idiomatic English. Label these rules and any English drafting examples as proposed adaptations. Show where a Hebrew construction has more than one workable English rendering and explain which one better preserves its function and rhythm. State the limits on generalizing from Hebrew journalism to independently composed English or to genres missing from the archive.

## Write one report

Include the following sections in a single Markdown file.

1. **Scope and coverage.** State the source, review date, method, and counts for inventoried, fully read, partly read, and excluded works, with duplicates accounted for. Include a compact article-by-article coverage appendix with canonical ID or path, language, genre, outlet or period where known, authorship status, and review disposition. Give the criteria for an attributable passage and note unresolved source issues.
2. **What characterizes Nim’s writing.** Present the strongest findings first. Include differences by genre, outlet, and era where supported, along with useful counterexamples and uncertainty. Separate common journalistic practice from decisions that appear especially characteristic within this archive. Treat claims of distinctiveness beyond the corpus as hypotheses unless the evidence can support them.
3. **מדריך כתיבה בעברית.** Give operational rules for drafting and revision. Explain what triggers each choice, how to execute it, and when it does not apply. Include examples for decisions about openings, story movement, interview framing, transitions, sentence rhythm, and endings where supported. Replace broad advice such as “be conversational” with observable choices about wording, placement, or structure.
4. **English writing guide.** Distinguish directly observed English habits, if any, from adaptations derived from Hebrew. Give instructions and short examples for English drafting and for translating Nim’s Hebrew, including the decisions that depend on genre or on the source passage. Label every example as a source excerpt, literal gloss, proposed translation, or original demonstration as appropriate.
5. **Evidence and validation.** Give each reusable rule a stable claim ID, its scope, strength of support, and locatable references. Cite the article ID, relative path, and a section heading, question, or short exact text anchor. Give multiple independent sources for strong rules where available, plus an exception when one matters. Include only the brief excerpts needed to verify the analysis. Report the held-out checks and any rules changed or dropped. Give each language a short editing checklist that points back to the rule IDs.
6. **Questions for Nim.** List only unresolved choices that would materially improve the guide, especially if a preference cannot be inferred reliably or if verified original English could change a conclusion. Leave questions about reconciling the separate reports for the later comparison.
